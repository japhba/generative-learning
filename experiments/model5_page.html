Model 5: practice binds the writer's own units, level by level

Simulation note, 18 November 2026 (UK time). Claude (Fable 5.2) for Jan Bauer. The runs this note replaces are in tex/model_5_appendix.tex.

The learner reads, judges and writes strings of a small grammar in which neighboring parts have to agree. Its reader and its judge are learned from teacher strings alone. Its writer has units of its own for the agreements, in two levels, and teacher strings do not bind them. Own samples do: each is judged, and the verdict goes to the units that wrote it. With the same teacher strings and the same number of own samples, this binds level 1 about {{lead1}} times and level 2 about {{lead2}} times sooner than classic wake-sleep. The upper level binds after the lower one without being told to, and a twin's samples do worse at the upper level. Inside the network the reason can be seen. A teacher string moves the top unit the wrong way or not at all, even when it is read as well as possible. An own sample moves it once the level below is bound, and the samples the judge refuses carry most of that. Two things the README expects do not happen: own samples are not read deeper than teacher strings, and with practice the reader's name for the top unit trails the writer. Four pieces of the learner are not local, its units are softmax units only, and a writer that takes its units from the reader needs no practice at all.

1. The world and the learner

World. A string is eight codes in four blocks, \(k_1k_2\,|\,k_3k_4\,|\,k_5k_6\,|\,k_7k_8\). There are 64 codes in 16 classes of four, \(c(k)\). Three rules make a string valid.

  • Blocks. The two classes of a block have to be one of 64 valid pairs out of 256. Every class has four partners on each side.
  • Level 1. At the cut between the first two blocks, a four-valued feature of the two classes has to agree, \(f_R(c(k_2))=f_L(c(k_3))=a\). The same holds at the cut between the last two blocks, with value \(b\). A quarter of the pairs of codes at a cut agree.
  • Level 2. The two values have to fit: \(b=a\) or \(b=a+1 \pmod 4\). Half of the pairs of values fit. This rule is a table with no feature of a single value behind it, so it cannot be read off single codes.

The teacher draws uniformly from the valid strings. A writer that knows the blocks and ignores the other rules writes 1 valid string in 32. The first run of section 2 uses a smaller world with one level: two blocks and one agreement with two values.

From teacher strings alone. A stored set of \(n\) teacher strings gives the learner everything it knows about the world, before any own sample.

  • Reader. Two small networks give every code a hidden vector of 16 numbers, one network for the two codes of a block and one for the two codes at a cut. A teacher pair raises the product of its two vectors and a shuffled pair lowers it: \[\Delta\mathbf{u}_k=\eta\,\big(t-\sigma(\mathbf{u}_k\cdot\mathbf{v}_{k'}+c)\big)\,\mathbf{v}_{k'},\qquad t=1\ \text{for a teacher pair},\ 0\ \text{for a shuffled one.}\]
  • Judge of a cut. The teacher's pair counts \(\mathbf{M}\) at the cuts, spread over codes with similar hidden vectors: \(p_E(k_2,k_3)=\big(\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{K}'^{\top}\big)_{k_2k_3}\). It refuses a pair when this is zero.
  • Judge of level 2. The reader groups the codes at a cut by their hidden vectors. The judge counts which two groups the teacher shows together at the two cuts, \(r_E\), relative to chance.
  • The writer's block part \(P_B(k,k')\): the teacher's blocks counted in the reader's groups of codes.

The writer's own units. A top unit \(z\) with four values chooses a unit at each cut, and each of these writes the two codes at its cut:

\[\begin{gathered}z\sim\mathrm{uniform},\qquad u_A\sim\mathbf{T}_A[z],\quad u_B\sim\mathbf{T}_B[z],\\ p(k_2\mid u)=\mathrm{softmax}\big(\log\mathbf{m}_R+\mathbf{d}_u\big),\qquad p(k_3\mid u)=\mathrm{softmax}\big(\log\mathbf{m}_L+\mathbf{d}'_u\big).\end{gathered}\]

\(\mathbf{T}_A,\mathbf{T}_B\) are softmax tables, \(\mathbf{m}_R,\mathbf{m}_L\) are what the block part writes at these places, and the same \(\mathbf{d}\) serve both cuts. All of these weights start small and random, so at the start every value of a unit writes the same.

The reader's naming layers have their own weights. Level 1 names a unit from the hidden vectors of the two codes, \(q(u\mid k_2,k_3)\). Level 2 names the top unit from the two units named below it, \(q(z\mid u_A,u_B)\).

Three rules. Each moves the weights between the units that were active, by a delta rule.

  • Wake. A teacher string is read by the naming layers, and the named units learn to write it: \(\Delta\mathbf{d}_u=\eta\,(\mathbf{e}_k-\mathbf{p}_u)\), and the same for the rows of \(\mathbf{T}_A,\mathbf{T}_B\).
  • Sleep. The naming layers learn to name the units that wrote an own sample.
  • Practice. An own sample gets one verdict per level, and each verdict goes to the unit directly above what it judges, as a third factor on the wake step: \[\begin{gathered}\delta_1=\log\frac{p_\theta(k_2,k_3)}{p_E(k_2,k_3)}\quad\text{at each cut, to its unit }u,\\ \delta_2=\log\frac{p_\theta(x_A,x_B)}{p_E(x_A)\,p_E(x_B)\,r_E}\quad\text{to the top unit }z,\\ \Delta\mathbf{w}=\eta\,\big(-(\delta-\bar\delta)\big)\,(\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{p}).\end{gathered}\] Here \(x_A,x_B\) are the pairs of codes at the two cuts. The level-2 verdict is given only when the judge accepts both cuts. Averaged over own samples, this is the gradient of \(\mathrm{KL}(p_\theta\,\Vert\,p_E)\).

A round is one batch of 64 fresh teacher strings for wake and one batch of 64 own samples. Classic wake-sleep uses the own samples for sleep. The learner with practice uses the same own samples for sleep and for practice. Every sample counts, teacher or own.

2. Practice against the same amount of wake and sleep

The README asks that in some window of training, own samples used for practice do more than the same number of samples spent on wake, on sleep, or on both. All losses are population losses in nats, exact, medians over {{worlds}} worlds.

One level

With one agreement and 1,024 stored teacher strings, the learner with practice is ahead of the best mix of wake and sleep from about 4,000 samples on. Before that it is behind, because practice first raises the loss.

  • Wake alone never binds the unit. The loss stays at {{one_w}} in every world.
  • The judge has to be right. With 256 teacher strings it accepts {{one_wrong256}} of the pairs that do not agree, and practice ends worse ({{one_wsp256}} against {{one_ws256}}). With 512 strings the lead is closing by the end: practice is ahead in {{one_512_worlds}} of {{worlds}} worlds.
  • Wake in turn is needed. Own samples alone diverge in {{sp_div}} of {{worlds}} worlds.
  • Sleep is needed. Without it the learner stops at {{wp_agree}} of pairs that agree.
  • The prior over the unit's values is fixed uniform. With a learned prior, classic wake-sleep collapses onto one value at these step sizes. At its best smaller steps it needs {{lp_ws}} samples to leave the plateau, against {{lp_wsp}} with practice.

4. Inside the network

This section looks at the units and at what single samples do to them, in the two-level learner with 4,096 teacher strings.

What the units come to stand for

  • The start is a saddle. All four values of a unit write the same, so nothing tells them apart.
  • Level-1 units take their values one after another. After 32 rounds a median of {{u32}} of the four units write one value with probability above 0.8, after 64 rounds all four. In the world shown, the last unit takes the value that is left.
  • The top unit sharpens after them, and ends in one of two codes: one table has one unit per row and the other two. It stands for the value at the first cut in {{code1}} worlds and at the second in {{code2}}.
  • A stuck world is one with a double. With practice no world ends that way here. Under wake-sleep {{ws_stuck}} worlds do, and in each of them two level-1 units write the same value.

Which sample moves which level

At each state of training the learner is copied, and one rule alone is applied at a small step. The table gives the change in the share of written strings that obey a level, per 1,000 samples, at states where that level is forming.

  • Teacher strings do not move the top unit. Read by the reader they push level 2 back ({{p_wake2}} per 1,000 samples, and forward in {{p_wake2_pos}} of the states). Read as well as possible, by the writer's own posterior, they give {{p_tied2}}. An own sample gives {{p_prac2}}.
  • So the top unit sits on a saddle under teacher strings. The exact step from a teacher string is close to zero there, however well the string is read. This is the README's point about saddles under teacher samples.
  • Refused samples carry more than accepted ones once a level has started to form. At level 2 they give {{p_ref2}} against {{p_acc2}}, and {{p_ref2_late}} against {{p_acc2_late}} when the level is further along. At level 1 it is {{p_ref1}} against {{p_acc1}}; at the very start of level 1 the two give the same ({{p_ref1_early}} and {{p_acc1_early}}).
  • Teacher strings and own samples do different jobs. Teacher strings lower the loss ({{l_wake}} nats per 1,000 samples) while they lower the share of valid strings: they keep the writer covering everything. Refused own samples raise the valid share and raise the loss ({{l_ref}}). This is why practice first raises the loss, and why it diverges without wake.
  • A twin's sample is worth little at these states. Credited by reading, an own sample gives {{p_or2}} at level 2 and a twin's {{p_tw2}}.
  • In the wake-sleep learner a teacher string read as well as possible does help level 2 ({{p_tied2_ws}}), but the reader's own reading does not ({{p_wake2_ws}}), and an own sample would give {{p_prac2_ws}}.

The upper level waits for the lower

  • The verdict is gated by the level below. While under 10% of strings obey level 1, an own sample changes level 2 by {{g0}} per 1,000 samples. At 10 to 30% it is {{g1}}, at 30 to 60% {{g2}}. The top unit acts on the string only through the units below it, so its verdict carries nothing until they are bound.
  • Teacher strings are read as well as own samples. At round 128 the agreement between reader and writer on the top unit is {{ag2_t}} for teacher strings and {{ag2_o}} for own samples (0 is chance, 1 is always); at level 1 it is {{ag1_t}} and {{ag1_o}}. Own samples are not read deeper. They reach the deep unit because the unit that wrote them is known, not because they are read better.
  • With practice, writing leads reading at level 2. At round 64 the writer is {{w64}} of the way at level 2 and the reader {{r64}}. What is ahead of the writer is the judge, which was complete before the first own sample.

What the network parts add

  • The judge accepts pairs it was never shown. With 256 teacher strings the stored set holds {{j_shown256}} of the valid pairs at a cut; the judge accepts {{j_acc256}} of the valid pairs and {{j_wrong256}} of the invalid ones. With 512 it holds {{j_shown512}} and the judge is exact. The hidden layer does this.
  • The naming layer needs the reader's hidden layer for reliability. Reading the codes directly, at its best sleep step, practice binds both levels in {{ab_wsp}} of {{worlds}} worlds and wake-sleep in {{ab_ws}}, against {{b_wsp_here}} and {{b_ws_here}}.

5. What is missing

The stronger baseline. A writer does not have to own the unit for an agreement. The judge of a cut is a small network, and its hidden layer holds the agreement's value. A writer that takes the unit from there is taught by teacher strings alone.

{{check_rows}}
Teacher strings2565121,0242,048

One-level world. The writer's tables are the teacher strings counted in the reader's units, which is what wake steps converge to as the step shrinks.

The README counts this as a stronger baseline that need not be beaten. The learner of this note is the one whose reader has units only for what the level above reads, so the writer has to own the rest.

Not local. Four pieces are not network operations: the judge pools stored teacher pairs over hidden-layer neighbors, with a width chosen on held-out strings; the reader's groups of codes come from a clustering pass; the writer's block part is counted in closed form; and the reader stops early on held-out strings.

Not a deep nonlinear network. Every map is one linear layer followed by a softmax, and the reader's hidden layer is linear.

Small. Two levels, units with four values, one table for the upper rule. Whether the lead keeps growing at a third level is not known.

Not every world binds. Practice binds both levels in {{b_wsp}} of 48 runs, {{b_twin}} of 48 from another start.

Level 1 is visible on the surface. Before level 1 is bound, own samples differ from teacher strings in a pair of neighboring codes.

The probes of section 4 measure what one rule does at a small step with the reader held still. They do not add up to the run, in which steps are large and the reader moves.

Not run: the control with a learner that differs only within a layer; a learner in which reader and units learn at the same time; a variational autoencoder; the mapping to learning in context.
