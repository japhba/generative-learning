Generative learning · model 5 · lateral verdict

Model 5: pair counts between shallow codes judge the next level, the prior names it

Simulation note, 16 November 2026 (UK time). Claude (Fable 5.2) for Jan Bauer.

The open point was how a verdict can depend on deep codes that have not been learned yet. Here it does not read them: the teacher's pair counts \(\mathbf{M}\) between shallow codes judge what a deep unit writes. Class units start as copies of the shallow codes and try further codes, and \(\mathbf{M}\) vetoes the wrong ones. With 4,096 teacher pairs this names all 16 hidden classes in {{locked}} of {{worlds}} worlds and lowers the population loss from {{start}} to {{full}} nats without a further teacher sample; a counted table of the same pairs stays at {{table}}. Credit goes to the units that read the own sample, not to those that wrote it, and practising on a twin's samples of the same quality ends at {{twin}}. There are three limits. Below about four teacher pairs per valid pair, practice makes the learner worse than the table. A reader that recalls through \(\mathbf{M}\) names the classes without any own sample. And at an equal number of samples, fresh teacher pairs counted in a table beat every mix of teacher and own samples.

1. The model

World

The world is one block of a hierarchy. Left and right of a cut, the shallow layer reads a code, \(k\) and \(k'\), each one of \(N=vm\) codes. A code stands for one production of the level below; the layer that reads it off the tokens is taken as learned. The codes fall into \(v\) classes of \(m\), \(c(k)\in\{1,\dots,v\}\): the symbol that the production belongs to. Of the \(v^2\) pairs of classes, \(vm\) are valid. They are the productions of the level above, with every class \(m\) times on each side. The population is uniform over the valid pairs of codes:

\[p^*(k,k')=\frac{1}{vm^3}\,\mathbb{1}\big[\,(c(k),c(k'))\ \text{valid}\,\big].\]

All runs use \(v=16\), \(m=4\): 64 codes per side and 1,024 valid pairs out of 4,096. The teacher set is \(n\) independent draws from \(p^*\), so a share \(\rho=1-e^{-n/1024}\) of the valid pairs has been shown. The learner is never told a class.

Learner

There are \(N\) class units on each side, \(a\) and \(a'\). A pair of units is drawn from a table, and each unit writes a code from its pool:

\[\begin{gathered}p_\theta(k,k')=\sum_{a,a'}T_{aa'}\,G^L_{ka}\,G^R_{k'a'},\\ \mathbf{G}^L=\operatorname{softmax}_k\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^L,\quad \mathbf{G}^R=\operatorname{softmax}_{k'}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^R,\quad \mathbf{T}=\operatorname{softmax}_{aa'}\boldsymbol{\Theta}.\end{gathered}\]

The reader is the transpose. It assigns a pair of codes to one pair of units, \((\hat a,\hat a')\sim q(a,a'\mid k,k')\propto T_{aa'}G^L_{ka}G^R_{k'a'}\). A lateral memory between the shallow codes counts the teacher's pairs, \(M_{kk'}\); it needs no class unit. All weights are bounded, \(|\Lambda|,|\Theta|\le s=6\). At the start unit \(i\) relays code \(i\): \(\Lambda_{ii}=s\), every other entry 0, and \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}=0\).

The prior is three properties of this form:

  • Independent children. Given the pair of units, the two codes are drawn independently. What a pair of units writes is a rectangle, pool times pool.
  • Spread. Every unit can try any code, and a weight that keeps being rewarded grows to the bound, so that all codes a unit keeps become equally likely.
  • Reuse. A unit has one pool for all the partners that \(\mathbf{T}\) gives it.

Watching and practice

Watching, one teacher pair \((k,k')\): \(M_{kk'}\) grows by one, and the weights of the most probable reading \((\hat a,\hat a')\) grow by one, \(\Lambda^L_{k\hat a}\), \(\Lambda^R_{k'\hat a'}\) and \(\Theta_{\hat a\hat a'}\). After watching the learner is close to a table of the pairs it was shown. It is not exactly one: every unit keeps a small chance for every other code, so about a fifth of what it writes is not valid, and its loss is above the table's.

Practice, one own sample: draw \((a,a')\sim\mathbf{T}\), \(k\sim G^L_{\cdot a}\), \(k'\sim G^R_{\cdot a'}\). The verdict is whether the teacher ever showed this pair, \(v=\mathbb{1}[M_{kk'}>0]\). The reader assigns the pair to \((\hat a,\hat a')\), and

\[\Delta\Lambda^L_{k\hat a}=\Delta\Lambda^R_{k'\hat a'}=\Delta\Theta_{\hat a\hat a'}=\eta\,(v-c\,\bar v),\]

with \(\bar v\) the running mean of the verdict, \(\eta=0.3\) and \(c=0.8\). No teacher sample is used.

The contrast \(v-c\,\bar v\) is what lets \(\mathbf{M}\) judge a deep choice. Suppose a unit tries a further code \(j\). If \(j\) is of the unit's class, the pair is valid with every partner of the unit and is accepted about as often as the unit's other samples, at a rate near \(\rho\). If \(j\) is of another class, it is accepted only with the partners that the two classes share. With \(c<1\) the first kind grows and the second is pushed out, and a valid pair that the teacher happened not to show does not undo a pool.

Scores, all computed exactly from the weights: the population loss \(\mathrm{KL}(p^*\,\Vert\,p_\theta)\) in nats; the share of classes that some unit holds entirely and purely; and the probability of the valid pairs that the teacher never showed, relative to \(p^*\).

2. What \(\mathbf{M}\) and the prior do

The figure runs the learner in the browser. Blue cells are valid pairs the learner writes, red cells invalid ones, dots the pairs the teacher showed.

The same four cases with 4,096 teacher pairs, {{worlds}} worlds and 2.1 million own samples each (Figure 2):

  • All three properties. The loss falls from {{start}} to {{full}} nats; the counted table of the same pairs is at {{table}}. All 16 classes are held by one unit each in {{locked}} of {{worlds}} worlds (at least {{locked_min}} of the classes in every world; {{locked_eff}} of {{worlds}} in the runs of section 3, which use other random numbers). {{valid}} of what the learner writes is valid. Valid pairs that the teacher never showed are written at {{full_new}} of their population rate, so the learner now knows more than the memory that judged it.
  • No verdict (every sample counts as accepted). Every unit takes every code and the loss goes to {{no_veto}} nats, the value for independent left and right codes. The prior alone merges everything.
  • No spread (a unit never tries another code). The learner stays a table: {{no_spread}} nats, and unshown pairs at {{no_spread_new}} of their population rate. The verdict alone merges nothing.
  • No reuse (one unit per shown pair, with two pools of its own). Within this many samples no class is named and the loss stays near the table's ({{private}} nats, unshown pairs at {{private_new}}). A unit with one partner can shape its rectangle around every missing pair.

Who gets the credit. In the rule above the verdict goes to the units that read the own sample. Giving it to the units that wrote the sample ends at the same loss ({{gen}} nats). The generator's code is not needed as a label.

The same model with one unit per class, trained by its likelihood gradient

This was the first learner tried: \(v\) units per side, a random start, a wake step on teacher pairs and a step against own samples that \(\mathbf{M}\) rejects. It does not find the classes. From a weak, a moderate and a sharp random start it ends at {{gw}} nats with teacher pairs only and at {{gp}} with half the samples its own (medians for the three starts), in no world below 0.05 (Figure 3, left). Own samples raise the share of valid output ({{gvw}} without them, {{gvp}} with them) without lowering the loss: units that mix classes stay mixed, and the learner avoids invalid pairs by writing fewer pairs.

This learner also tests the idea that own samples propagate deeper. Let a unit pair learn only if its drive, \(T_{aa'}G^L_{ka}G^R_{k'a'}N^2\), exceeds 1, that is, if the read explanation is more probable than a chance pair. From a sharp random start, {{gel_o}} of own samples pass and {{gel_t}} of teacher samples (Figure 3, right). With teacher pairs only the learner then keeps its random code ({{gth_w}} nats); with own samples it ends at {{gth_p}}. So the asymmetry exists and own samples help under a threshold, but both are far worse than the same learner without a threshold. From a weak or moderate start nothing passes the threshold at all.

3. What it buys, and what it does not

Against the size of the teacher set

Population loss in nats, median over {{worlds}} worlds. Counted table: the teacher's counts with the best uniform smoothing. Practice: 2.1 million own samples after watching. Gradient learner: one unit per class, wake only, rehearsing the stored set.

  • A window. From 4,096 to 16,384 teacher pairs practice ends below the table, by a factor of {{x4096}} at 4,096 pairs ({{p4096}} against {{t4096}} nats) and of {{x16384}} at 16,384. The table needs about three times as many teacher pairs for the same loss.
  • Below the window practice hurts. At 2,048 pairs the loss after practice is {{p2048}} against {{t2048}} for the table. Too many valid pairs are still unshown, the verdict rejects them, and the learner narrows.
  • Above the window the table catches up ({{t32768}} against {{p32768}} at 32,768): the bounded weights leave a floor near 0.02 nats.
  • Recall through \(\mathbf{M}\) does it without own samples. This reader lets code \(j\) join the unit of code \(k\) if \(\mathbf{M}\) has seen \(j\) with most of the partners of \(k\). It uses the same statistic as practice, for all codes at once, and ends at or below practice from 4,096 pairs on ({{r4096}} and {{r8192}} nats at 4,096 and 8,192). It fails below that as well.

At an equal number of samples

Teacher and own batches were interleaved at a fixed share (Figure 5). The learner's best share of own samples is 0 at small budgets and between 0.5 and 0.9 at large ones, but a counted table fed the same total number of teacher pairs is better at all {{totals}} budgets: {{ht13}} against {{h13}} nats at \(2^{13}\) samples, {{ht22}} against {{h22}} at \(2^{22}\). With a share of own samples of 0.997 or more the learner ends worse than without practice, because it practises against a nearly empty \(\mathbf{M}\).

Whose samples

After 4,096 teacher pairs the learner practised on its own samples, or on the samples of a twin: the same learner with a teacher set of its own, practising on its own samples at the same time, so that its samples are as good as the learner's (the twin ends at {{twin_kl}} nats). Credit is by reading in both cases. On own samples the learner ends at {{own}} nats, on the twin's at {{twin}}, and own is ahead in {{own_wins}} of {{worlds}} worlds (Figure 6). The twin's samples show the learner which pairs the twin writes wrongly, not which the learner does. The same number of fresh teacher pairs, simply counted, ends at {{table_end}}. Fed to the practice rule instead of being counted, fresh teacher pairs do worse than own samples ({{teacher_end}} nats): they are almost always accepted, so nothing new is tried.

4. Three levels

A string is now four codes, \(k_1k_2\,|\,k_3k_4\). Each half is the block above. The 64 valid class pairs are the productions of 16 symbols of a second level, and the top has 64 valid pairs of those symbols: 262,144 valid strings. Three learners get the same number of teacher strings:

  • A table of whole strings.
  • All strings first, then practice. While the strings were shown no class unit could read them, so nothing about the second level was kept and the two halves stay independent.
  • Practice between two halves of the strings. The second half is read into the class units and their pairs are counted, which is the same lateral memory one level up.

With 16,384 strings the table of strings is at {{d_flat}} nats, strings first at {{d_first}}, practice in between at {{d_between}}; with 131,072 strings {{d_flat2}}, {{d_first2}} and {{d_between2}} (Figure 7). Practice moves the frontier by one level and no further: the counts for the next level can only be taken from teacher samples that arrive after the classes are named. The remaining loss of the third learner is a naming error. Practice leaves several units with the same pool, and reading them as one unit is not yet reliable.

What this shows

  • A verdict on a deep choice does not need deep units. Pair counts between shallow codes separate a code of the right class from a code of another class.
  • The verdict and the prior have different jobs: without the verdict everything merges, without spread nothing does, and without reuse no unit becomes a class.
  • Credit by the reader's own reading is enough.
  • Own samples beat equally good foreign ones, because they show the learner its own wrong pairs.

What it does not show

  • An advantage over more teacher samples. Per sample, own samples are worth far less.
  • That own samples are needed. Recall through \(\mathbf{M}\) uses the same statistic without generating.
  • A learner that starts from a weak random code. The classes form from units that start as copies of the shallow codes; the likelihood-gradient learner from a random start does not find them.
  • Robustness. Practice hurts when fewer than about four teacher pairs per valid pair were seen; \(c=0.8\) was chosen from a scan over 0.6 to 0.9 on four worlds; several units end with the same pool and are not merged.
  • A deeper stack. The three-level run names one level and counts the next.
