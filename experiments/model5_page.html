Generative learning · model 5 · lateral verdict

Model 5: pair counts between shallow codes judge the next level, the prior names it

Simulation note, 16 November 2026 (UK time). Claude (Fable 5.2) for Jan Bauer.

The open point was how a verdict can depend on deep codes that have not been learned yet. Here it does not read them: the teacher's pair counts \(\mathbf{M}\) between shallow codes judge what a deep unit writes. Class units start as copies of the shallow codes and try further codes, and \(\mathbf{M}\) vetoes the wrong ones. With 4,096 teacher pairs this names all 16 hidden classes in {{locked}} of {{worlds}} worlds and lowers the population loss from {{start}} to {{full}} nats without a further teacher sample; a counted table of the same pairs stays at {{table}}. Credit goes to the units that read the own sample, not to those that wrote it, and practising on a twin's samples of the same quality ends at {{twin}}. There are three limits. Below about four teacher pairs per valid pair, practice makes the learner worse than the table. A reader that recalls through \(\mathbf{M}\) names the classes without any own sample. And at an equal number of samples, fresh teacher pairs counted in a table beat every mix of teacher and own samples. A follow-up (section 6) explains the last limit and where it ends: in one block no teacher pair is lost on the learner, so an own sample cannot be worth a fresh one; with three levels a budget split between teacher and own strings ends far below the same budget of teacher strings. Section 8 repeats the block with networks: the hidden layer of a plain network has the classes long before its output writes them, and practice in which the network's proposals are judged through that hidden layer beats the same number of fresh teacher pairs in one block from 4,096 teacher pairs on. Section 9 removes the memory of judged pairs and then the backpropagation: the writer learns from the judge's answers through its weights alone, and networks trained by a local rule keep the hidden layer but write poorly however many teacher pairs they get. Section 10 asks whose proposals are needed and adds a third level: a twin with the same teacher pairs and another random start supplies proposals that serve as well or better, and with three levels the hidden layers hold both levels long before anything is written well.

1. The model

World

The world is one block of a hierarchy. Left and right of a cut, the shallow layer reads a code, \(k\) and \(k'\), each one of \(N=vm\) codes. A code stands for one production of the level below; the layer that reads it off the tokens is taken as learned. The codes fall into \(v\) classes of \(m\), \(c(k)\in\{1,\dots,v\}\): the symbol that the production belongs to. Of the \(v^2\) pairs of classes, \(vm\) are valid. They are the productions of the level above, with every class \(m\) times on each side. The population is uniform over the valid pairs of codes:

\[p^*(k,k')=\frac{1}{vm^3}\,\mathbb{1}\big[\,(c(k),c(k'))\ \text{valid}\,\big].\]

All runs use \(v=16\), \(m=4\): 64 codes per side and 1,024 valid pairs out of 4,096. The teacher set is \(n\) independent draws from \(p^*\), so a share \(\rho=1-e^{-n/1024}\) of the valid pairs has been shown. The learner is never told a class.

Learner

There are \(N\) class units on each side, \(a\) and \(a'\). A pair of units is drawn from a table, and each unit writes a code from its pool:

\[\begin{gathered}p_\theta(k,k')=\sum_{a,a'}T_{aa'}\,G^L_{ka}\,G^R_{k'a'},\\ \mathbf{G}^L=\operatorname{softmax}_k\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^L,\quad \mathbf{G}^R=\operatorname{softmax}_{k'}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^R,\quad \mathbf{T}=\operatorname{softmax}_{aa'}\boldsymbol{\Theta}.\end{gathered}\]

The reader is the transpose. It assigns a pair of codes to one pair of units, \((\hat a,\hat a')\sim q(a,a'\mid k,k')\propto T_{aa'}G^L_{ka}G^R_{k'a'}\). A lateral memory between the shallow codes counts the teacher's pairs, \(M_{kk'}\); it needs no class unit. All weights are bounded, \(|\Lambda|,|\Theta|\le s=6\). At the start unit \(i\) relays code \(i\): \(\Lambda_{ii}=s\), every other entry 0, and \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}=0\).

The prior is three properties of this form:

  • Independent children. Given the pair of units, the two codes are drawn independently. What a pair of units writes is a rectangle, pool times pool.
  • Spread. Every unit can try any code, and a weight that keeps being rewarded grows to the bound, so that all codes a unit keeps become equally likely.
  • Reuse. A unit has one pool for all the partners that \(\mathbf{T}\) gives it.

Watching and practice

Watching, one teacher pair \((k,k')\): \(M_{kk'}\) grows by one, and the weights of the most probable reading \((\hat a,\hat a')\) grow by one, \(\Lambda^L_{k\hat a}\), \(\Lambda^R_{k'\hat a'}\) and \(\Theta_{\hat a\hat a'}\). After watching the learner is close to a table of the pairs it was shown. It is not exactly one: every unit keeps a small chance for every other code, so about a fifth of what it writes is not valid, and its loss is above the table's.

Practice, one own sample: draw \((a,a')\sim\mathbf{T}\), \(k\sim G^L_{\cdot a}\), \(k'\sim G^R_{\cdot a'}\). The verdict is whether the teacher ever showed this pair, \(v=\mathbb{1}[M_{kk'}>0]\). The reader assigns the pair to \((\hat a,\hat a')\), and

\[\Delta\Lambda^L_{k\hat a}=\Delta\Lambda^R_{k'\hat a'}=\Delta\Theta_{\hat a\hat a'}=\eta\,(v-c\,\bar v),\]

with \(\bar v\) the running mean of the verdict, \(\eta=0.3\) and \(c=0.8\). No teacher sample is used.

The contrast \(v-c\,\bar v\) is what lets \(\mathbf{M}\) judge a deep choice. Suppose a unit tries a further code \(j\). If \(j\) is of the unit's class, the pair is valid with every partner of the unit and is accepted about as often as the unit's other samples, at a rate near \(\rho\). If \(j\) is of another class, it is accepted only with the partners that the two classes share. With \(c<1\) the first kind grows and the second is pushed out, and a valid pair that the teacher happened not to show does not undo a pool.

Scores, all computed exactly from the weights: the population loss \(\mathrm{KL}(p^*\,\Vert\,p_\theta)\) in nats; the share of classes that some unit holds entirely and purely; and the probability of the valid pairs that the teacher never showed, relative to \(p^*\).

2. What \(\mathbf{M}\) and the prior do

The figure runs the learner in the browser. Blue cells are valid pairs the learner writes, red cells invalid ones, dots the pairs the teacher showed.

The same four cases with 4,096 teacher pairs, {{worlds}} worlds and 2.1 million own samples each (Figure 2):

  • All three properties. The loss falls from {{start}} to {{full}} nats; the counted table of the same pairs is at {{table}}. All 16 classes are held by one unit each in {{locked}} of {{worlds}} worlds (at least {{locked_min}} of the classes in every world; {{locked_eff}} of {{worlds}} in the runs of section 3, which use other random numbers). {{valid}} of what the learner writes is valid. Valid pairs that the teacher never showed are written at {{full_new}} of their population rate, so the learner now knows more than the memory that judged it.
  • No verdict (every sample counts as accepted). Every unit takes every code and the loss goes to {{no_veto}} nats, the value for independent left and right codes. The prior alone merges everything.
  • No spread (a unit never tries another code). The learner stays a table: {{no_spread}} nats, and unshown pairs at {{no_spread_new}} of their population rate. The verdict alone merges nothing.
  • No reuse (one unit per shown pair, with two pools of its own). Within this many samples no class is named and the loss stays near the table's ({{private}} nats, unshown pairs at {{private_new}}). A unit with one partner can shape its rectangle around every missing pair.

Who gets the credit. In the rule above the verdict goes to the units that read the own sample. Giving it to the units that wrote the sample ends at the same loss ({{gen}} nats). The generator's code is not needed as a label.

The same model with one unit per class, trained by its likelihood gradient

This was the first learner tried: \(v\) units per side, a random start, a wake step on teacher pairs and a step against own samples that \(\mathbf{M}\) rejects. It does not find the classes. From a weak, a moderate and a sharp random start it ends at {{gw}} nats with teacher pairs only and at {{gp}} with half the samples its own (medians for the three starts), in no world below 0.05 (Figure 3, left). Own samples raise the share of valid output ({{gvw}} without them, {{gvp}} with them) without lowering the loss: units that mix classes stay mixed, and the learner avoids invalid pairs by writing fewer pairs.

This learner also tests the idea that own samples propagate deeper. Let a unit pair learn only if its drive, \(T_{aa'}G^L_{ka}G^R_{k'a'}N^2\), exceeds 1, that is, if the read explanation is more probable than a chance pair. From a sharp random start, {{gel_o}} of own samples pass and {{gel_t}} of teacher samples (Figure 3, right). With teacher pairs only the learner then keeps its random code ({{gth_w}} nats); with own samples it ends at {{gth_p}}. So the asymmetry exists and own samples help under a threshold, but both are far worse than the same learner without a threshold. From a weak or moderate start nothing passes the threshold at all.

  • Above the window the table catches up ({{t32768}} against {{p32768}} at 32,768): the bounded weights leave a floor near 0.02 nats.

At an equal number of samples

Teacher and own batches were interleaved at a fixed share (Figure 5). The learner's best share of own samples is 0 at small budgets and between 0.5 and 0.9 at large ones, but a counted table fed the same total number of teacher pairs is better at all {{totals}} budgets: {{ht13}} against {{h13}} nats at \(2^{13}\) samples, {{ht22}} against {{h22}} at \(2^{22}\). With a share of own samples of 0.997 or more the learner ends worse than without practice, because it practises against a nearly empty \(\mathbf{M}\).

Whose samples

After 4,096 teacher pairs the learner practised on its own samples, or on the samples of a twin: the same learner with a teacher set of its own, practising on its own samples at the same time, so that its samples are as good as the learner's (the twin ends at {{twin_kl}} nats). Credit is by reading in both cases. On own samples the learner ends at {{own}} nats, on the twin's at {{twin}}, and own is ahead in {{own_wins}} of {{worlds}} worlds (Figure 6). The twin's samples show the learner which pairs the twin writes wrongly, not which the learner does. The same number of fresh teacher pairs, simply counted, ends at {{table_end}}. Fed to the practice rule instead of being counted, fresh teacher pairs do worse than own samples ({{teacher_end}} nats): they are almost always accepted, so nothing new is tried.

4. Three levels

A string is now four codes, \(k_1k_2\,|\,k_3k_4\). Each half is the block above. The 64 valid class pairs are the productions of 16 symbols of a second level, and the top has 64 valid pairs of those symbols: 262,144 valid strings. Three learners get the same number of teacher strings:

  • A table of whole strings.
  • All strings first, then practice. While the strings were shown no class unit could read them, so nothing about the second level was kept and the two halves stay independent.
  • Practice between two halves of the strings. The second half is read into the class units and their pairs are counted, which is the same lateral memory one level up.

With 16,384 strings the table of strings is at {{d_flat}} nats, strings first at {{d_first}}, practice in between at {{d_between}}; with 131,072 strings {{d_flat2}}, {{d_first2}} and {{d_between2}} (Figure 7). Practice moves the frontier by one level and no further: the counts for the next level can only be taken from teacher samples that arrive after the classes are named. The remaining loss of the third learner is a naming error. Practice leaves several units with the same pool, and reading them as one unit is not yet reliable.

What this shows

  • A verdict on a deep choice does not need deep units. Pair counts between shallow codes separate a code of the right class from a code of another class.
  • The verdict and the prior have different jobs: without the verdict everything merges, without spread nothing does, and without reuse no unit becomes a class.
  • Credit by the reader's own reading is enough.
  • Own samples beat equally good foreign ones, because they show the learner its own wrong pairs.

What it does not show

  • An advantage over more teacher samples in one block. Per sample, own samples are worth far less there (section 6 for three levels).
  • That own samples are needed. Recall through \(\mathbf{M}\) uses the same statistic without generating.
  • A learner that starts from a weak random code. The classes form from units that start as copies of the shallow codes; the likelihood-gradient learner from a random start does not find them.
  • Robustness. Practice hurts when fewer than about four teacher pairs per valid pair were seen; \(c=0.8\) was chosen from a scan over 0.6 to 0.9 on four worlds; several units end with the same pool and are not merged.
  • A deeper stack. The three-level runs name one level and count the next.

6. Follow-up: where own samples beat fresh teacher samples, and why one block cannot show it

Added 16 November 2026. Sections 1 to 5 are as they were; where this section corrects them, it says so.

In one block an own sample cannot be worth a fresh teacher pair, because no teacher pair is lost on the learner. With three levels part of every teacher string is lost until the classes are named, and there a budget split between teacher and own strings ends far below the same budget of teacher strings.

Why one block cannot show it

  • The judge knows what the writer knows. After watching, \(\mathbf{M}\) and the table \(\mathbf{T}\) that the generator starts from hold the same pairs. The verdict confirms an own sample only if the teacher showed that pair, and then the generator already writes it.
  • Nothing in a teacher pair is lost. Both codes are read by the shallow layer and \(\mathbf{M}\) stores the pair whole. A fresh pair always adds something about the world; an own sample can only reorganise what \(\mathbf{M}\) holds. For own samples to win at an equal number of samples, part of a teacher sample has to be unreadable for the learner at its current stage.
  • Own samples do not reach deeper than teacher pairs in this learner. The class units start as copies of the codes, so a teacher pair is read into them as easily as an own sample. The asymmetry of section 2 ({{gel_o}} of own against {{gel_t}} of teacher samples) existed only under a threshold that made the learner worse.

What naming the classes is worth, and what a verdict gets of it

Against the same teacher set, reorganising does buy something: a table over pairs of classes has 64 valid entries to estimate, the table over pairs of codes 1,024.

  • A pooled verdict gets more. A written code is judged against everything that its partner unit stands for: \[\begin{gathered}v_L=\mathbb{1}\Big[\sum_{k''\in\mathcal{P}(\hat a')}M_{kk''}>0\Big],\qquad v_R=\mathbb{1}\Big[\sum_{k''\in\mathcal{P}(\hat a)}M_{k''k'}>0\Big],\\ \Delta\Lambda^L_{k\hat a}=\eta\,(v_L-c\,\rho_L),\quad \Delta\Lambda^R_{k'\hat a'}=\eta\,(v_R-c\,\rho_R),\quad \Delta\Theta_{\hat a\hat a'}=\eta\,(v_Lv_R-c\,\bar v).\end{gathered}\] \(\mathcal{P}(a)\) is the pool of unit \(a\), the codes it writes at least half as often as its most frequent one. \(\rho_L\) and \(\rho_R\) are the rates at which the same verdict accepts the teacher's pairs (stored pairs replayed from \(\mathbf{M}\), their own count left out), so an own sample is held to \(c\) times what a true sample gets; \(\bar v\) is the running mean of \(v_Lv_R\) on own samples; \(\eta=1\), \(c=0.8\). With 4,096 teacher pairs, practice needs {{ob_own_below}} own samples before it is below the table of those pairs, and a table of that many fresh teacher pairs is far ahead (section 3). A larger step (\(\eta=1\) instead of 0.3, used here) brings the number of own samples for 0.1 nats from about a million to about a third of a million; more exploration by the units, or a table imprinted at the first sighting of a pair, made practice fail or end worse.

Three levels at a fixed budget of strings

The world is that of section 4: strings of four codes, 262,144 valid strings. A budget of \(B\) strings is split into \((1-f)B\) teacher strings and \(fB\) own strings. The learner watches half of its teacher strings, practises on its own strings (one pair for each half of the string, with the pooled verdict), then reads the other half of the teacher strings through its class units and counts pairs of named pairs. With \(f=0\) nothing is named, and the pairs of pairs it counts are whole strings: a table of strings. The runs use {{ob_dworlds}} worlds in which no two classes have the same partners.

Two corrections to section 4. The naming error reported there had two causes that are not about learning. First, practice leaves two or three of the 64 units per side with a pool that mixes two classes, and reading a code by its largest pool let those units merge the two classes one level up. Here a code is read as the group of like units with the most posterior mass, which gives exactly 16 names per position once practice has run for half a million own strings. Second, in 5 of the first 64 worlds two classes have the same partners. Within a block such classes are one class and can only be told apart one level up, so those worlds are left out here.

What this changes, and what stands

  • Changed: a split budget beats teacher samples only once the world has three levels. The condition is that part of a teacher sample cannot be read before practice.
  • Stands: own samples are not needed as samples. A pass over \(\mathbf{M}\) without sampling names the classes from fewer teacher pairs. For sampling to be needed, the memory that judges would have to be too large to go through entry by entry.
  • Stands: practice is slow, about half a million own strings per level.
  • Not tested: four levels, where a table of strings is out of reach of any budget; practice at the top level (here the top is counted, not named); a start that is not a copy of the codes.

7. How deep a teacher sample and an own sample reach, over the course of learning

Added 16 November 2026.

In the learner of this note, own samples reach further than teacher samples only while few teacher strings have been seen, and the gap closes once practice has named the classes. The top level is reached by teacher strings, and only after the naming; own samples never reach it, because practice runs within the blocks. The lead that the README expects for own samples does appear in the likelihood-gradient learner from a sharp random start. There it is gone after {{pr_gcross}} samples or, if only samples read above chance may learn, teacher samples stay locked out and the learner gets worse.

A sample reaches level \(\ell\) if the reader's explanation of it is more probable than chance at \(\ell\) and at every level below:

  • level 1: the units that read the codes hold them above chance, \(N\,G_{k\hat a}>1\), for every code of the sample;
  • level 2: in addition, the pair of units is above chance in the table, \(K^2\,T_{\hat a\hat a'}>1\), with \(K\) units per side;
  • level 3 (three levels only): in addition, the string read through the class names falls on a pair of named pairs that the counted teacher strings also fall on.

Levels 1 and 2 are averaged over the reader's posterior \(q(a,a'\mid k,k')\), so they give the probability that the winner the rule credits passes. This is the drive of section 2, taken level by level. Teacher samples are fresh draws from the population. Own samples are the learner's own pairs, one from each block, as in practice.

The two learners: the learner of section 6 (pooled verdict, \(\eta=1\)) on three levels, {{pr_sworlds}} worlds, which watches half of the teacher strings and then practices; at every checkpoint the other half is read through the current names and counted at the top. And the likelihood-gradient learner of section 2 on one block, 16 units per side, each starting as a copy of one random code, with \(\mathbf{M}\) filled from 8,192 teacher pairs and teacher and own batches alternating, {{pr_gworlds}} worlds.

  • With few teacher strings, own strings reach further at first. With 4,096 teacher strings, {{pr_ao2}} of own and {{pr_at2}} of teacher strings reach level 2 after watching, and early practice widens the gap ({{pr_ao2m}} against {{pr_at2m}} after \(2^{16}\) own strings). Own pairs are written where the table is strong. A pair the teacher showed only once sits below chance: every sighting raises its weight by the same step and the table is exponential in the weights, so the pairs seen often take most of its mass. After naming both are near the top ({{pr_ao2e}} and {{pr_at2e}} at \(2^{20}\) own strings).
  • With more teacher strings there is no lead. With 16,384 teacher strings, teacher strings reach level 2 more often than own strings after watching ({{pr_bt2}} against {{pr_bo2}}).
  • The top is reached by teacher strings, once the classes are named. Level 3 rises from {{pr_at30}} to {{pr_at3e}} of teacher strings with 4,096 of them and from {{pr_bt30}} to {{pr_bt3e}} with 16,384, in step with the share of classes named. Before the naming the top holds whole strings, and a fresh string is almost never among them.
  • The README's picture appears in the gradient learner, and does not last. From the sharp random start, {{pr_go1}} of own and {{pr_gt1}} of teacher samples reach level 1. When every sample learns, the pools spread out and the reach of both falls (own samples to {{pr_go1min}} after {{pr_go1min_at}} samples); then the two rise together, from {{pr_gcross}} samples on teacher samples reach level 1 about as often as own ones or more, and at the end both are near {{pr_gt1e}} (loss {{pr_gloss}} nats). When only samples read above chance learn (the threshold of section 2), teacher samples never exceed {{pr_gt1max1}}, and the loss ends at {{pr_gloss1}} nats.

So in neither learner do own samples open a level that teacher samples cannot reach afterwards. Practice changes the reader, and then teacher samples reach as far as own ones. The one level where reach decides the loss is the top of the stack, and there teacher strings get through, using the names that practice built.

8. With networks: the hidden layer has the classes before the output writes them

Added 17 November 2026. Runs: experiments/model5_network_baseline.py, experiments/model5_network_practice.py.

A plain network trained on the teacher pairs has the classes in its hidden layer from 2,048 pairs on, while its output writes about 16 times worse than that hidden layer allows. Practice in which the writer proposes pairs, the reader's hidden layer judges them against the teacher pairs and the answer is kept closes most of this gap without a further teacher pair. From 4,096 teacher pairs on it beats the same number of fresh teacher pairs, in one block. Whose proposals are judged does not matter, and a verified pair used as one more sample is worth at most a teacher pair.

The network, and what watching gives it

The network has two linear layers with 16 hidden units: a code is mapped to a hidden vector, and the hidden vector to the partner,

\[p_\theta(k,k')=\operatorname{softmax}_{kk'}\big(\mathbf{u}_k\cdot\mathbf{v}_{k'}+a_k+b_{k'}\big).\]

It is trained by backpropagation on 85% of the teacher pairs; the other 15% choose the weight decay and the step at which training stops. The table shows its loss in two readings: what its output writes, and what its hidden layer holds, measured by grouping the codes by their hidden vectors (16 groups per side) and counting the same teacher pairs over pairs of groups.

Judge and practice

There are two networks of this form with separate weights, a reader and a writer, each trained as above from its own random start. The judge is the reader's hidden layer together with the stored teacher pairs \(\mathbf{M}\). Two codes are neighbours if their hidden vectors (mean removed) have a cosine of at least \(\tau\), and every stored pair is spread evenly over the pairs of its neighbours:

\[p_E=\frac{1}{n}\,\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_L\,\mathbf{M}\,\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_R^{\top},\qquad K_{kj}=\mathbb{1}\big[\cos(\mathbf{u}_k,\mathbf{u}_j)\ge\tau\big],\]

with \(\tilde{\mathbf{K}}\) the neighbourhood matrix with columns normalised to one. \(\tau\) is the width of this estimate and is chosen by the likelihood of the held-out teacher pairs. The judge's answer to a pair is \(p_E(k,k')\): zero if the teacher showed no pair of neighbours, otherwise how often it showed them.

Practice. The writer is trained on a memory \(\mathbf{C}\) that starts as the teacher's counts, \(\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{M}\). In a round the writer proposes pairs by drawing them from its own output; for every proposed pair the judged count replaces the raw one, \(C_{kk'}\leftarrow n\,p_E(k,k')\); then the writer is fitted to \(\mathbf{C}\) again, as in watching. A sample is one proposal. No teacher pair is drawn during practice.

9. A writer that learns through its weights, and a local rule

Added 17 November 2026. Runs: experiments/model5_network_practice.py (curves with the online arms, local, curves --rule local).

The writer can learn from the judge's answers through its weights alone, one step per proposal with nothing kept, and a step in which only the two written codes move does almost as well. From 8,192 teacher pairs on this is ahead of the same number of fresh teacher pairs; it needs about four times as many proposals as keeping the answers. The networks can also be trained by a local rule. Their hidden layer then holds the classes as before, but what they write stops near 0.2 nats however many teacher pairs they are given. Practice takes such a network far below that. A table that simply counts the teacher pairs is local too, and it stays ahead of or level with the local network after practice.

The writer learns through its weights

There is no memory of judged pairs. For every proposal \(x=(k,k')\) drawn from the writer, the judge returns \(p_E(x)\) and the writer takes one step that moves its own log-probability of \(x\) towards the log of the answer:

\[\delta=\log p_\theta(x)-\log\max\big(p_E(x),\,\varepsilon\,\bar e\big),\qquad \theta\leftarrow\theta-\eta\,\delta\,\frac{\mathbf{g}}{\Vert\mathbf{g}\Vert^2},\quad \mathbf{g}=\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(x).\]

\(\bar e\) is the running mean of the positive answers, so an answer of zero counts as a small fraction \(\varepsilon=10^{-3}\) of a typical one; \(\eta=0.5\) is the share of the error at \(x\) that one step removes; \(\delta\) is cut off at \(\pm5\). In a second version only the weights of the two written codes move, each hidden vector by the error times the other one, and the part of \(\mathbf{g}\) that comes from the normaliser is left out:

\[\mathbf{u}_k\leftarrow\mathbf{u}_k-s\,\mathbf{v}_{k'},\quad \mathbf{v}_{k'}\leftarrow\mathbf{v}_{k'}-s\,\mathbf{u}_k,\quad a_k\leftarrow a_k-s,\quad b_{k'}\leftarrow b_{k'}-s,\qquad s=\frac{\eta\,\delta}{\Vert\mathbf{u}_k\Vert^2+\Vert\mathbf{v}_{k'}\Vert^2+2}.\]

A local rule for the networks themselves

So far the two networks were trained by backpropagation with an exact normaliser. A local rule for the same network: for a teacher pair \((k,k')\), and for \(\nu\) contrast pairs \((k,k'')\) whose right code is taken from another teacher pair,

\[g=y-\sigma\big(\mathbf{u}_k\cdot\mathbf{v}_{k'}+c\big),\qquad \mathbf{u}_k\leftarrow\mathbf{u}_k+\eta\,g\,\mathbf{v}_{k'},\quad \mathbf{v}_{k'}\leftarrow\mathbf{v}_{k'}+\eta\,g\,\mathbf{u}_k,\]

with \(y=1\) for the teacher pair and \(y=0\) for a contrast pair. A step touches the two active codes only, and no sum over all pairs is taken. What such a network writes is \(p(k,k')\propto p_L(k)\,p_R(k')\,e^{\mathbf{u}_k\cdot\mathbf{v}_{k'}}\). The number of contrast pairs (1, 4 or 16) and of passes over the teacher pairs are chosen by the likelihood of held-out teacher pairs under what it writes.

What this shows, and what it does not

  • Locality and what the network gives recognition go together. A rule that touches only the two active codes gives the same hidden layer and the same judge.
  • Locality does not give a deep layer that teacher samples fail to reach. The plateau at small weights is short, and teacher pairs end it. What a local rule does leave behind is the writing: the output stays far from what the hidden layer holds, and more teacher pairs do not close that.
  • Practice closes it, with a local step. The step uses the judge's answer, the writer's own confidence, and the hidden vectors of the two written codes.
  • Not local: drawing a proposal needs a competition over all pairs; the judge compares hidden vectors across codes; the width of its neighbourhoods and the stopping points are chosen on held-out teacher pairs.
  • Not shown: an advantage over a learner that counts the teacher pairs, a difference between own and foreign proposals, and anything beyond one block.

10. Whose proposals, and three levels

Added 17 November 2026. Runs: experiments/model5_network_practice.py (curves --onesided with the arms online[_local] followed by _twinsame, _judge, _mix, _mix_twinsame, _uniform, _reward) and experiments/model5_network_depth.py (watching, saddle, curves); figures and numbers: experiments/model5_network_source_figures.py.

Whose proposals the writer practises on decides only where the judge is asked, and the writer's own proposals are not the best place to ask. With the README's twin (the same teacher pairs, another random start), own and twin proposals are level for networks trained by backpropagation, and the twin's are better for networks trained by the local rule. Proposals drawn from the judge's own estimate, which need no writer, do as well or better. Own proposals lead only late, and only with backpropagation. With three levels the hidden layers hold both levels from 8,192 teacher strings, where a table of strings has learned nothing, and what the networks write is much further behind them than in one block. Practice with a local step lowers the loss of the local-rule network where fresh teacher strings barely do. But a twin's proposals are ahead of own ones in every world, and the network trained by backpropagation on the same strings writes better with no practice at all.

Whose proposals, in one block

The README now asks that a twin's samples do worse than the learner's own, the twin being a second learner that starts from other random weights. In sections 8 and 9 the twin also had teacher pairs of its own; here it is given the same teacher pairs. The writer of section 9 practises on proposals from different sources. Judge, answer and step are the same throughout, only the source of the proposals changes:

  • own: drawn from the writer;

Why the source matters the way it does

A step at a proposed pair \(x\) lowers \(\log p_\theta(x)\) by \(\eta\,\delta(x)\), with \(\delta=\log p_\theta-\log p_E\). Leave aside what the step does to other pairs through shared weights, and measure the writer by its cross-entropy to the judge's estimate, \(L=-\sum_x p_E(x)\log p_\theta(x)\). One step at \(x\) changes it by \(\eta\,\delta(x)\,(p_E(x)-p_\theta(x))\), so for proposals drawn from a source \(q\)

\[\mathbb{E}_{x\sim q}\big[\Delta L\big]=-\eta\sum_x q(x)\,\big(p_\theta(x)-p_E(x)\big)\,\log\frac{p_\theta(x)}{p_E(x)}\;\le\;0 .\]

Every term is negative or zero, so a question helps wherever it is asked. The source enters only through \(q(x)\), how often \(x\) is asked about. That separates two kinds of error:

  • A valid pair that the writer hardly writes (\(p_\theta\ll p_E\)) is repaired at a rate proportional to \(q(x)\). Own proposals ask about it rarely, for the very reason that it needs repair. The judge's draws ask as often as it should occur, and so does a twin whose gaps lie elsewhere.
  • An invalid pair that the writer writes (\(p_E=0\)) is removed at a rate proportional to \(q(x)\) too. The judge's draws never ask about it, a twin only if it writes it as well, own proposals always.

So own proposals are the right ones for one part of the loss only. The loss splits exactly into that part and the rest,

\[\mathrm{KL}\big(p^*\,\Vert\,p_\theta\big)=\underbrace{-\log\textstyle\sum_{x\ \mathrm{valid}}p_\theta(x)}_{\text{invalid mass}}\;+\;\underbrace{\mathrm{KL}\big(p^*\,\Vert\,p_\theta(\cdot\mid\mathrm{valid})\big)}_{\text{unevenness among the valid}} ,\]

and after watching the second part is the larger one. After 8,192 teacher pairs the invalid mass is {{s10_bw0}} of the writer's {{s10_b0}} nats with backpropagation and {{s10_lw0}} of {{s10_l0}} with the local rule; with three levels it is {{s10_dw0}} of {{s10_d0}}.

  • How much a twin helps depends on how unlike its gaps are. Before practice, the log-probabilities that learner and twin give the valid pairs correlate at {{s10_cb}} with backpropagation and at {{s10_cl}} with the local rule ({{s10_cd}} with three levels). With backpropagation the two networks are uneven in the same places, so the twin asks what the learner would ask; with the local rule they are uneven in different places, and the twin asks about the learner's gaps.
  • The invalid mass is not removed faster by own proposals either, except late with backpropagation. With the local rule the twin's proposals leave less of it ({{s10_ltw}} against {{s10_low}} nats after 32,768 proposals in one block; {{s10_dtw}} against {{s10_dow}} after 16,384 with three levels), although the twin writes only {{s10_ov}} of the learner's invalid mass itself. A step at one invalid string lowers the strings of the same kind through the shared weights, so the twin need not write the same strings. With backpropagation own proposals do remove more ({{s10_bow}} against {{s10_btw}}), and that is the late lead above.
  • Proposals drawn from the judge leave the invalid mass in place, as the formula says: unchanged in one block with backpropagation, and rising with three levels ({{s10_dw0}} to {{s10_djw}} nats).

Three levels: what watching gives

Strings of four codes \((k_1k_2\,|\,k_3k_4)\) as in section 4: two blocks \(L=(k_1,k_2)\), \(R=(k_3,k_4)\); each of 16 symbols has four valid class pairs, and 64 of the 256 pairs of symbols are valid. There are 262,144 valid strings among 16.8 million. The network is the network of section 8 with a second level on top, with separate weights for the two halves \(h=0,1\):

\[\begin{gathered}s_h(k,k')=\mathbf{u}_k\cdot\mathbf{v}_{k'}+a_k+b_{k'},\qquad \mathbf{e}_h(k,k')=\mathbf{A}_h^\top\big(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_k\otimes\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{k'}\big),\\ p(L,R)=\operatorname{softmax}_{L,R}\Big(s_0(L)+s_1(R)+\mathbf{e}_0(L)\cdot\mathbf{e}_1(R)\Big).\end{gathered}\]

\(\mathbf{u}_k,\mathbf{v}_{k'}\) are the hidden vectors of the two codes of a block (16 units), \(\mathbf{e}_h\) is the hidden vector of the block (16 units), computed from the outer product of the two code vectors scaled to unit length. The local rule is the rule of section 9 at both levels: at level 1 the two codes of a block against the same left code with a right code from another teacher string; at level 2 the two blocks of a string against the same left block with the right block of another teacher string,

\[g=y-\sigma\big(\mathbf{e}_0(L)\cdot\mathbf{e}_1(R)+c\big),\qquad \mathbf{A}_0\leftarrow\mathbf{A}_0+\eta\,g\,\big(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k_1}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{k_2}\big)\,\mathbf{e}_1(R)^\top,\quad \mathbf{A}_1\ \text{likewise}.\]

Level 2 does not change level 1. As a reference the same network is trained by backpropagation on the likelihood of the teacher strings. Read out means: codes grouped by their hidden vectors into 16 classes per position, the class pairs that were shown grouped by the mean hidden vector of their blocks into 16 symbols per side, and pairs of symbols counted. The judge is built as in section 8, level by level: a block is accepted if a neighbouring block was shown, and the teacher strings are spread over the strings of neighbouring accepted blocks.

Three levels: practice

Reader and writer are two networks trained by the local rule from separate random starts; the reader is kept at the pass at which it tells held-out teacher strings best from shuffled ones, the writer at the pass at which it writes them best. For every proposed string the judge gives three answers, one per block, \(p_E(L)=\sum_R p_E(L,R)\) and \(p_E(R)\), and one for the pair of blocks. Each level takes the local step of section 9 towards its own answer:

\[\begin{aligned}\text{level 1:}\quad&\delta=\log\operatorname{softmax}_L s_0(L)-\log p_E(L),\quad\text{the two codes of the block move as in section 9;}\\ \text{level 2:}\quad&\delta=\mathbf{e}_0(L)\cdot\mathbf{e}_1(R)+b-\log\frac{p_E(L,R)}{p_E(L)\,p_E(R)},\\ &\mathbf{A}_0\leftarrow\mathbf{A}_0-s\,\big(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k_1}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{k_2}\big)\,\mathbf{e}_1(R)^\top,\quad \mathbf{A}_1\ \text{likewise},\quad b\leftarrow b-s .\end{aligned}\]

As in section 9, \(s\) is \(\eta\,\delta\) divided by the squared length of what the step multiplies, so that one step removes the share \(\eta\) of the error. Level 2 steps only if both blocks are accepted. The sources are those of the one-block run; one more draws the two blocks from the writer's level 1 and combines them at random.

What it would take for own samples to do better than a twin's

  • Something about an own sample must matter that is not in the sample. In the network learner the judge sees the string and the writer steps at the string, so two sources differ only in where their strings fall. That cannot favour the writer's own strings, except for its invalid mass.
  • The first learner of this note did show it (section 3: {{own}} against {{twin}} nats for a twin's samples). There an own sample was a trial of a class unit, a unit writing a code it had not written before, and the verdict was credited to units. A twin's samples are trials of the twin's units.
  • That learner needed its trials because teacher samples could not train its units directly. A network's hidden layer is trained by teacher samples with a local rule, at both levels. The property that makes the network attractive is the one that removes the need for own samples.
  • Two ways out, both outside what was built here. A writer with chosen deep units whose learning rule uses which unit was active when the sample was written (a three-factor rule; the README's note of 15 November counts the sample's cause as ruled out if it is used as a label for recognition, and this would be a different use of it). Or a comparison in which invalid mass is what counts, for instance a writer that over-generalises after watching.
