Learning from an incomplete example

A learner watches someone move a labeled triangle, then invents strings of moves to practice on. When it omits a difficult move, its reader can reach a deeper operation. In this model, that makes it better at writing and reading fully worked teacher strings.

What is one observation?

A string records where the triangle ended and which moves were made. The starting arrangement \(z\) is hidden. Each field arrives as a pair of tokens. Multiply the tokens within each pair, then apply the two decoded moves to the final arrangement to recover the start.

What the writer draws

The six elements of \(S_3\) are the arrangements of the three labels. Here \(e\) copies, \(R\) rotates by \(120^\circ\), and \(S\) reflects. The rule \(SRS=R^{-1}\) makes the order of the moves matter.

Draw the root uniformly. Choose the lower and upper moves \(g_1,g_2\), then write downward with their inverses to obtain the final arrangement \(y\). Reading undoes this construction.

\[ y=z\,g_2^{-1}g_1^{-1}, \qquad z=y\,g_1g_2. \]

For each field \(h\), independently draw a uniform mask \(u\) and write \((u,u^{-1}h)\). Changing the masks in the example changes its appearance while preserving its entire read. The fixed-rule teacher generates \(6^4=1{,}296\) equally likely strings from the root and the three masks.

Both sources have identical single-token marginals. A two-token detector can distinguish a copy from a rotation by their product. In the broader model, each level also has a visible context \(c_j\) and its own learned rule for that context. The plots use one context.

How does a wrong string improve a weight?

Watching learns a local verdict. A bank of synapses counts observed context–move pairs at every level. The teacher uses a deterministic rule, so a covered row can judge an attempted move. An unseen row abstains. Meanwhile the reader and writer update their separate weights only at blocks the reader reaches.

Practice spends that verdict at depth. The writer discards its trajectory after generating the string. The reader starts from the visible tokens. A copy passes its state through; an executed move transmits with probability \(r\). If the source executes with probability \(p\), the next block is reached with probability

\[h(p,r)=(1-p)+pr.\]

At \(p=r=1/2\), the upper block is reached on \(3/4\) of own strings and \(1/2\) of teacher strings. When a binary choice is rejected, the other choice is identified. Either verdict therefore updates a reached true entry by \(s\gets s+\eta(1-s)\), in both pathways. A later move can receive credit even when an earlier move was wrong.

What counts as improvement?

For the developed teacher, \(p_j\) is the writer’s probability of its correct move and \(r_j\) the reader’s chance of passing it. The writer pays forward population KL on every teacher string. The reader reports the hidden root on a fresh teacher string, guessing uniformly if the parse breaks.

\[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L&=-\log(p_1p_2),\\ \Pr(\hat z=z)&=\tfrac16+\tfrac56 r_1r_2 . \end{aligned}\]
Why these learning curves can be computed exactly

In this binary model, every primed string trains the first block. Its strength after \(t\) updates is \(s_t=1-(1-s_0)(1-\eta)^t\). The deeper block receives independent Bernoulli updates with known, time-dependent probabilities \(r_1(t)\) on teacher strings and \(h(p_1(t),r_1(t))\) on own strings. Propagating the distribution of the update count gives the expected loss and accuracy at each budget, including the uncertainty from Watching. The plot uses \(\eta=0.01\) and starts all four probabilities at \(1/2\).

The extra probability of reaching the second block on an own string is \((1-p_1)(1-r_1)\). Couple the two learners using the same uniform random draw for that block. Each teacher update also occurs on the own arm, and the own arm sometimes receives an additional update. This proves strictly lower expected KL at every finite positive budget after priming. Classic sleep spends a sample updating only the reader, and even a perfect teacher-directed sleep update cannot increase first-block reader strength faster than fresh wake here. All-wake therefore bounds the writer improvements of any fresh-wake/classic-sleep schedule in this binary comparison.

Does it matter whose string it is?

Add a third, three-way operation. Give two primed learners lower-level strengths \((1/3,5/6)\) and \((5/6,1/3)\) and a uniform terminal choice. The writers now have equal population KL, equal validity, and the same distribution of the number of executed lower moves. They differ in where an omission occurs.

For either recipient, the terminal block is initially reached on \(217/324\) of its own strings, \(34/81\) of its twin’s, and \(5/18\) of the teacher’s. Exchanging the two lower strengths therefore costs exactly \(1/4\) in terminal reach at equal source quality.

The live twin changes as the two learners train on each other’s strings. The KL-matched swap instead exchanges the recipient’s current lower writer strengths on every draw and keeps its terminal row, enforcing equal proposal KL throughout.

Which parts depend on the architecture?

The construction assumes a local detector for a word product, a reliable copy path, and a parser that loses its state after a failed move. The early advantage holds for this bounded parser and local update rule. A reader that can train every deep row directly from a teacher string can match or beat practice. Whether a generic MLP learns these circuits remains open.

Matching the full distribution of visible strings would force identical expected updates for a fixed reader. The surface control here matches individual tokens, leaving their products free to differ. Offline learning transfers information acquired during Watching from the judge into the writer and reader weights.
