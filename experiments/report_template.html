Exploration · Feedback · Identifiability

What generation can add

A controlled investigation of how exploratory trajectories expose corrective behavior that expert demonstrations leave unobserved.

Exploration plus corrective feedback removes a persistent recovery-error floor. The effect replicates in two LLM families and has an exact, two-weight Gaussian counterpart. Random and frozen-model exploration reach the same endpoint, pointing to informative state coverage as the mechanism.

01 / Experimental result

The benefit survives a change of model

We fully fine-tuned Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B-Base (596,049,920 parameters) and HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M (134,515,008 parameters), using three training seeds each. Models control a simple tracking system. Expert demonstrations show how to remain on track; exploratory actions expose errors and receive oracle corrections.

Every exploratory endpoint matches the oracle on the sampled episodes. The oracle has a small positive error because bounded actions sometimes require more than one step to recover. Every main arm retains perfect nominal tracking at every measured checkpoint.

The improvement is not immediate. At 512 labels, current-model exploration temporarily increases mean recovery error to 13.01 in Qwen and 3.68 in SmolLM2. Later improvement and saturation do not establish distinct scaling exponents.

02 / Task and intervention

Learning to stay on track, and to return

Each step presents requested motion u ∈ {−2, −1, 0, 1, 2} and tracking error e. The LLM selects an action a ∈ {−3, …, 3}, encoded as one of seven legal tokens. The simulator performs the arithmetic.

enext = e + a − u
a* = clip(u − e, −3, 3)

Expert acquisition

Every training episode starts at e = 0. The expert chooses a = u, so error stays zero. Only five distinct inputs appear, no matter how often those demonstrations are repeated.

Exploratory acquisition

Sampled actions generate nonzero errors. The oracle labels each visited state with its corrective action. Current-model exploration visits 109–115 inputs in Qwen and 124–142 in SmolLM2; random exploration visits 237–238.

For example, with u = 1 and e = 2, copying the requested motion gives a = 1 and preserves the error. The corrective action a = −1 returns error to zero. Zero-error demonstrations never supply this corrective example.

Every arm uses supervised cross-entropy. Generation runs without gradients and determines which states receive labels. All visited states are labeled, including mistakes; there is no success filtering. This is constrained action generation with corrective supervision, rather than unrestricted text generation or policy-gradient RL.

Each round adds 512 new labels and 512 replay examples, then takes 16 updates of batch size 64. All arms share the same requested-motion stream, warm model, warm optimizer state, label counts, and update counts within a seed. Model-based sampling uses temperature 1. The task prompt explicitly supplies the dynamics, objective, and action mapping.

The test deliberately perturbs initial state. Recovery begins at an error uniformly sampled from ±1 through ±3, outside expert demonstration coverage. Larger-shift evaluation uses ±4 through ±8. Fresh command sequences are disjoint from training sequences, but individual numeric state inputs can recur.

03 / A mechanism with actual weights

One linear layer is enough

A conditional Gaussian learner makes the missing direction explicit. It has just two weights, no hidden layer, and no activation. Labels are noisy linear teacher outputs; the prior is standard Gaussian.

x = (u, e),   w* = (1, −1)
y = xᵀw* + ε,   ε ∼ N(0, σ²),   σ = 0.25
w ∼ N(0, I)

Σn = [I + XᵀX / σ²]⁻¹
μn = Σn Xᵀy / σ²

With expert inputs (u, 0), the corrective weight never enters the likelihood. Its posterior mean remains zero and its variance remains one. The same fact appears in a squared-loss gradient:

∂L/∂we ∝ (prediction − label) × e = 0

Supervised data still teach the motion coefficient. The obstruction is specifically the absent error direction. Exploration exposes that direction, makes the input Gram matrix full rank, and lets teacher feedback identify the corrective coefficient.

The corresponding LLM behavior

For each warm and final LLM, a fixed 29-input probe fits decoded action to intercept + b × u + c × e where the ideal action is unsaturated. Warm and expert-only policies get 5/29 inputs correct. Every exploratory final policy gets 29/29 correct and has fitted coefficients (0, 1, −1).

Weak excitation changes the floor

Adding process noise with standard deviation 0.05 exposes small errors even under expert control. At 1,024 labels, expert weight error falls to 0.024403, versus 0.000336 for learner exploration. The missing direction becomes weakly observed rather than entirely absent.

The Gaussian simulation uses continuous actions, teacher-label noise 0.25, exploration noise 0.5, and 16-step resets. Posterior updates are exact and were checked against independent batch calculations. Its probabilistic grounding is conditional Gaussian regression; it is not an RBM training result or a claim of Bayesian optimality over all known physical facts.

04 / Interpretation

The evidence supports a coverage mechanism

Established in this experiment

  • Exploratory actions plus informative feedback teach recovery missing from expert trajectories.
  • The endpoint result replicates across two model families and all six training seeds.
  • A single linear layer exposes an exact connection between input coverage, posterior uncertainty, and weight error.
  • Nominal tracking remains intact in every main condition.

Still open

  • An advantage unique to adaptive self-generation: random and frozen-model exploration work too.
  • Different scaling exponents or an advantage at every finite budget.
  • A quantitative mapping from Gaussian weight-learning rates to transformer learning rates.
  • A corresponding human effect, or a general advantage over learning from the same information.

The useful claim is that generation can act as data augmentation by changing which informative states the learner encounters. Corrective feedback supplies the missing labels. Matching the acquired observations would remove this particular information difference.

The setup is related to DAgger (Ross, Gordon & Bagnell, 2011), which labels learner-induced states, and on-policy language-model distillation (Agarwal et al.). This investigation isolates a small, interpretable instance of that mechanism.

Compute is a separate axis

The main comparison matches labels and optimizer updates. Current-model generation adds approximately 50% more counted training/acquisition tokens. Recorded training/generation phases average 44.47 versus 39.27 seconds in Qwen, and 21.78 versus 19.18 seconds in SmolLM2, for current-model versus expert acquisition. These timings exclude several overheads and were measured on a shared node; they are not total wall-clock or equal-compute comparisons.

Effect sizes and supplemental exact policy evaluation

At the primary endpoint, the paired current-model-minus-expert difference is −2.19417 for Qwen (95% seed-bootstrap interval −2.48242 to −1.93872) and −2.58879 for SmolLM2 (−2.62939 to −2.52124). All six paired seed differences favor exploratory feedback.

A supplemental lookup evaluation integrates over the IID command distribution by dynamic programming. Its 85-input domain is verified closed under every final policy, and lookup actions match all 294,912 recorded final evaluation actions. Expected recovery error is 2.26379 for Qwen expert-only and 2.69925 for SmolLM2 expert-only. Every exploratory policy reaches the oracle's expected error of 0.01750.

The initial, overly broad lookup grid changed batch padding and flipped a near-tied SmolLM2 expert action. The strict agreement check caught this. Matching the original numerical convention on the closed domain resolved it, with all recorded actions verified. Failures and correction records are retained; original sampled-episode results remain primary.

  • Independent analysis regraded 110,592 trajectories and checked oracle labels, replay indices, matched command streams, and optimizer counts.
  • Fresh test command sequences have zero overlap with training sequences.
  • Gaussian posterior means and covariances passed 16,896 independent batch-formula checks.
