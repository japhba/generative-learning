Generative learning · toy model 3 · the Oracle on a chain

A learner that can check a chain long before it can write one, and closes the gap by practice

The world is a set of chains of {{n}} states in which every state has to fit both of its neighbours. Checking a chain needs one table of allowed pairs. Writing a chain in a single pass needs more: a tree of tables that say, for the two ends of a span, which state lies in the middle. The Oracle, a learner made only of such tables, fills all of them from the same teacher chains by the same counting rule. Its pair table is complete after a few hundred chains, while its top table needs about {{lead3}} times as many. Practice closes the gap without further teacher chains: the Oracle joins pieces it already holds, writes the result in one pass, checks every link against its pair table, and counts what passes.

Summary

  1. The setup. {{S}} states, each with {{m}} allowed successors; the population is the valid chains of {{n}} states. The Oracle is our name for the learner: a recogniser (one table of pairs) and a generator (a tree of three tables, for spans of 2, 4 and 8 links). It learns by counting, either from teacher chains (watching) or from chains it has assembled, written and checked itself (practice). All results are over 16 random worlds.
  2. Recognition leads, on equal footing. The same chains and the same counting rule fill the recogniser {{lead1}}, {{lead2}} and {{lead3}} times sooner than the generator's three levels. A counting argument predicts {{lead_theory1}}, {{lead_theory2}} and {{lead_theory3}}, and the delay grows with the length of the span in four other worlds as predicted (section 2).
  3. Practice closes the gap without teacher data. After 4,096 teacher chains the Oracle accepts every link but writes only {{n12_w3}} of the population's spans of 8 links. Practice alone brings that to {{n12_p3}}. Counted in teacher chains, half of the spans are written after {{half_practice}} chains instead of {{half_watch3}} (section 3). The practised tables converge to the exact ones (section 7).
  4. Reading and writing rise together, level by level. In practice, spans of 2 links come first, then 4, then 8 (half-way after {{stair12_1}}, {{stair12_2}} and {{stair12_3}} episodes). A span the Oracle can write is also one it holds as a single unit and can check in one lookup, so its reading grows with its writing (section 4).
  5. Watch first, then practise. Practice pays in a window: too few teacher chains and the pair table has gaps that practice cannot fill, too many and watching alone suffices. The same examples in the order watch-then-practise give {{sched_watch_first}}, alternating {{sched_alternate}}, practise-then-watch {{sched_practise_first}} (section 5).
  6. Blind practice does not beat fresh teacher chains. Token for token, spending the whole budget on fresh teacher chains was best at every budget we tried. Practice is what the Oracle can do when no more teacher chains come. Practice does beat fresh chains if the Oracle looks each join up first and writes only those it lacks: nine in ten of the spans of 8 links then take {{aim_u_aim_t90}} tokens instead of {{aim_u_fresh_t90}}. That selection is put in by hand and pays only if a look-up costs almost nothing (section 5).
  7. Supervising every level adds nothing. A teacher who supplies only the pair table does as well as one who fills every level. With a teacher who slips, a recogniser that accepts every pair it sees ruins the generator ({{noisy_trust_own}} of own chains valid), and a careful one keeps it ({{noisy_left_own}}). Once the generator has copied the teacher's slips, judging examples cleans it, and it matters little whether the examples judged are its own or the teacher's (section 6).
  8. Own against foreign examples. In a world of 16 regions, an Oracle taught in one region can take in a fresh chain only if the chain touches a state it knows. Nothing else is given to it. Chains of the population mostly touch nothing it knows and are lost: {{reach_lost_b0}} of them for good if no step leads from one region to another, and the first few thousand if 1 state in 64 has such a step. Practice is never lost, but it never enlarges what the Oracle reads. Token for token, practice writes more of the home region's spans of 8 links than fresh chains of the population do ({{reach_own_home3_23}} against {{reach_fresh_home3_23}}). For the whole population, fresh chains win as soon as they connect (section 8).

1. The model

World

There are \(S\) states. Each state has \(m\) allowed successors, which gives a table \(\mathbf{T}\) of allowed pairs, \(T_{ab}=1\) if \(b\) may follow \(a\). The population is the set of chains \(\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\) in which every link \((x_t,x_{t+1})\) is allowed, with a uniform first state and a uniform choice among the allowed successors at every step. So every state has to fit the one before it and the one after it. The main world has \(S\) = {{S}}, \(m\) = {{m}} and \(n\) = {{n}}, so a chain has 8 links.

The Oracle

The Oracle is the learner. It holds tables of counts and nothing else, which makes it the reference against which a network version can later be compared.

  • Recogniser. A pair table \(\hat{\mathbf{T}}\): how often \(b\) was seen right after \(a\). A link passes once its pair has been seen. A chain passes if all of its links do. This is one lookup per link and one AND, at any length.
  • Generator. A binary tree with one table per level. A node holds the two ends \((a,b)\) of a span of \(2k\) links and draws the state \(c\) in the middle from its table. Its two children then hold \((a,c)\) and \((c,b)\). With \(k=4,2,1\) the tree writes the 9 states in one top-down pass, with no loop. A node without an entry for its ends guesses uniformly.

The table a node of half-length \(k\) should hold is the bridge of the chain,

\[\begin{gathered}p_k(c\mid a,b)=\frac{T^{k}(c\mid a)\,T^{k}(b\mid c)}{T^{2k}(b\mid a)},\\ T^{2k}(b\mid a)=\sum_c T^{k}(c\mid a)\,T^{k}(b\mid c),\end{gathered}\]

where \(T^{k}(c\mid a)\) is the probability of being at \(c\) after \(k\) steps from \(a\). The recogniser needs \(\mathbf{T}\) only. The generator needs its powers. They are fixed by \(\mathbf{T}\), but a network that writes in one pass has to store them.

Two ways of learning, one counting rule

  • Watching. A teacher chain adds its 8 adjacent pairs to the recogniser. It also adds every span it contains to the generator: for each \(k\) and each position, a count for (ends, midpoint). This is the maximum-likelihood fit of every table to the supplied chains, the Oracle's version of an autoencoder trained end to end.
  • Practice. No teacher data. One episode picks a level \(k\) at random, takes a unit of \(k\) links that the Oracle holds, \(a\to c\), and a second one that starts where the first ends, \(c\to b\). The Oracle writes the \(2k+1\) states in one pass, checks every link with the recogniser, and adds a count for \((a,b)\to c\) if all links pass. A unit of one link is an accepted pair. A unit of \(2k\) links is a span for which the level-\(k\) table has an entry.

Why this is not learning from what the Oracle already believes. Practice applies the same counting rule as watching, to the Oracle's own chains. A learner that counts plain samples of its own generator learns nothing on average. Here the span is assembled from the levels below and filed in the level above, so the upper table is trained on what the lower tables imply, and that is the second formula above. All information comes from the pair table, which came from the teacher.

What is measured

All scores are on 8,000 held-out population chains per world. A span of the population is written if one pass of the generator from its two ends gives a chain with allowed links only (judged by the world's \(\mathbf{T}\)). It is read as a unit if the Oracle holds an entry for its ends, so that it could check a chain that shows only those two states. A link is accepted if its pair is in the recogniser.

The counting argument

A chain shows \(n-1\) of the \(Sm\) allowed pairs, and \(n-2k\) of about \(S\,m^{2k}\) spans of \(2k\) links. After \(N\) chains the shares seen at least once are about

\[\begin{gathered}q(N)=1-e^{-N(n-1)/(Sm)},\\ r_k(N)=1-e^{-N(n-2k)/(S\,m^{2k})},\end{gathered}\]

so the generator's level \(k\) is half full later than the recogniser by a factor \(m^{2k-1}(n-1)/(n-2k)\). Practice needs every link of a span to be accepted, which happens for a share of about \(q^{2k}\) of the spans.

4. Reading and writing rise together, level by level

  • The levels arrive in order. Spans of 2 links are half written after {{stair12_1}} practice episodes, spans of 4 after {{stair12_2}}, spans of 8 after {{stair12_3}}. Nothing schedules this. A level can only join units that the level below has produced.
  • Writing a span makes it readable as a unit. The share of spans held as one unit and the share written in one pass never differ by more than {{read_write_gap}} in any run (the difference is an occasional lucky guess of a midpoint). In the Oracle they are the same table.
  • The order is a ratchet. Links are accepted first, from the teacher. Checked links certify written spans of 2 links. Those become units of 2 links, which the next level joins, and so on. Recognition is ahead at the bottom only. Above it, each level of reading is made by writing at that level.
  • What the larger units are good for. A chain that shows only every second, fourth or eighth state can then be checked with one lookup per shown link. Before practice the Oracle could check such a chain only where it had watched the very same span.
  • Different routes, one unit. A unit is filed under its two ends. In this world {{multi_route3}} of the spans of 8 links have ends that more than one route joins, and the Oracle holds each of them as a single unit with several midpoints. It finds this out by writing the routes, not by seeing them side by side in the teacher's chains.

5. How much to watch, how much to practise

  • Practice needs a period of watching first. With the same examples, watching and then practising ends at {{sched_watch_first}} of the spans of 8 links, alternating in 64 blocks at {{sched_alternate}}, practising and then watching at {{sched_practise_first}}. Practice on an empty pair table has nothing to join.
  • Teacher chains and practice replace each other only in one direction. Along the bottom row of the heat map (no practice), {{half_watch3}} teacher chains are needed for half of the spans. With a few million practice episodes, {{half_practice}} are. No amount of practice makes up for a pair the teacher has not shown.
  • Token for token, fresh teacher chains beat blind practice. With a budget of \(2^{19}\) tokens, spending all of it on teacher chains gives {{split19_all}}, half of it {{split19_half}}. The whole budget for the teacher was best at all four budgets (in {{split15_wins}}, {{split17_wins}}, {{split19_wins}} and {{split21_wins}} of 16 worlds). A teacher chain of 9 states brings spans at every level and new pairs. A practice episode brings one span, and even an episode at the top level, which also writes 9 states, brings only one.

So the benefit of blind practice is not that own examples are better than the teacher's. It is that they cost no teacher.

Practice with a look-up: skipping what the Oracle already holds

In everything above the Oracle practises blindly. It draws a join in proportion to how often it has seen the two parts, and it writes the join out even if its table already holds it. As it learns, a growing share of its episodes repeats what it can already do. The same holds for the teacher's chains. The Oracle cannot choose those, but it could choose its own. Here it looks a drawn join up first, and writes and checks it only if its table lacks it. Only written joins count as tokens; a look-up does not. A new join is filed with the count that blind practice would give it in the long run. This selection is put in by hand. Nothing in the Oracle's own sampling steers it towards the joins it lacks.

  • With the look-up, own examples beat fresh teacher chains. With all steps equally likely, the Oracle writes half of the population's spans of 8 links after {{aim_u_aim_t50}} tokens and nine in ten after {{aim_u_aim_t90}}. Fresh teacher chains alone need {{aim_u_fresh_t50}} and {{aim_u_fresh_t90}}, and blind practice needs {{aim_u_blind_t50}} for the half. Fresh chains are ahead only up to about {{aim_u_cross}} tokens.
  • The advantage grows as the Oracle improves. The share of a teacher's chains that shows something new falls with every chain, and every written own example is new. Practice stops by itself when its look-ups find nothing new, here after {{aim_u_stop_used}} tokens with {{aim_u_stop_w3}} of the spans written.
  • It is larger when some steps are rare. If one of the two steps is taken nine times as often as the other, most long chains still contain a rare step, and teacher chains show the rare combinations rarely. After \(2^{20}\) tokens the Oracle fails on {{aim_r_fresh_miss_20}} of the population's spans of 8 links with fresh chains, and on {{aim_r_aim_miss_20}} with the look-up.
  • The practice moves up the levels by itself. At first {{aim_u_lvl2_first}} of the written tokens go to joins for spans of 4 links. Once those are held, look-ups there find nothing new, and the tokens go to spans of 8 links. No schedule is set.
  • A budget is then best spent on few teacher chains and much practice. Of \(2^{21}\) tokens, the best split gives {{asplit_u_21_best}} to fresh teacher chains and reaches {{asplit_u_21_aim}}. The whole budget for the teacher gives {{asplit_u_21_fresh}}, which is worse in {{asplit_u_21_wins}} of 16 worlds.

What this rests on. A look-up must cost almost nothing next to writing a chain. This run made about {{aim_u_looks}} look-ups per written token. If every look-up cost one token, nine in ten of the spans would take {{aim_u_aim_t90_alt}} tokens, more than the {{aim_u_fresh_t90}} of fresh chains. The teacher here draws its chains from the population. A tutor who knew the Oracle's gaps and showed only those would do as well as practice. And practice still cannot supply a pair that the teacher has not shown.

6. Supervision at every level, and a teacher who slips

Section 3 already showed one half of this: a teacher who fills every level of the generator achieves nothing that a teacher who supplies only the pair table does not. Here the teacher also slips: 3% of the states in its chains are replaced by random ones. Three learners watch the same chains and then practise.

  • Trusting every pair is costly. A recogniser that accepts a pair seen once has accepted {{noisy_trust_wrong}} wrong pairs after 8,192 chains, next to the {{pairs_total}} right ones. Practice builds on them: only {{noisy_trust_own}} of the Oracle's own chains of 9 states are valid.
  • A careful recogniser protects practice. Accepting a pair only when seen twice leaves {{noisy_forced_wrong}} wrong pairs, and {{noisy_forced_own}} of own chains are valid after practice.
  • Letting the teacher fill every level costs a little. The teacher's slips go straight into the generator's tables: before practice only {{noisy_forced_own0}} of own chains are valid. Practice dilutes them. Leaving the levels to practice ends at {{noisy_left_own}}, higher in {{noisy_left_wins}} of 16 worlds.
  • Care costs teacher chains. Half of the spans of 8 links are written after {{noisy_left_half}} chains by the careful learners, {{noisy_trust_half}} by the trusting one.

Whose examples to judge

Sampling from its own generator shows the Oracle what it writes wrongly, as often as it writes it. It cannot show what the Oracle is unable to write. In the clean world all of the Oracle's own chains are valid, so own samples have nothing to find. Here the generator has wrong entries: it has copied 16,384 chains of the teacher who slips, slips included, and {{judge_start_own3}} of its own chains of 9 states are valid. The recogniser accepts a pair seen twice and is then left as it is ({{judge_wrong_pairs}} wrong pairs next to the {{pairs_total}} right ones). The generator then gets \(2^{21}\) further tokens in one of four ways. Every chain shown or written is counted, and nothing is looked up.

  • Teacher chains, taken as shown. The generator files every span of every chain.
  • Teacher chains, judged. A span is filed only if the recogniser accepts all of its links.
  • Own samples, judged. The Oracle draws a chain from its own generator in one pass. If the recogniser accepts every link, each entry that wrote the chain is counted once more.
  • Own samples, judged, wrong entries dropped. In addition, an entry loses a count when the state it wrote fits neither of its neighbours.
  • More chains from the same teacher, taken as shown, never help. Its slips enter at the rate at which its right spans do. {{judge_teacher_own3}} of own chains stay valid.
  • Judging is what cleans the generator. Own chains are valid in {{judge_teacher_judged_own3}} of cases after judged teacher chains, in {{judge_own_own3}} after judged own samples, and in {{judge_own_drop_own3}} if wrong entries are also dropped.
  • Whose examples are judged matters little. The teacher seeded the generator, so the generator's mass lies where the teacher's chains fall, and either kind of example reaches its wrong entries equally often.
  • Only the teacher's chains add coverage. Of the population's spans of 8 links, {{judge_teacher_judged_write3}} are written after the judged teacher chains and {{judge_own_write3}} after the judged own samples ({{judge_start_write3}} at the start).

What an advantage of own samples would need. The generator's mass would have to lie where the teacher's chains are rare, with a recogniser that is still right there, and with some of the generator's samples there passing. A table has no such bias: where it holds nothing it guesses among all {{S}} states. A generator that generalises would have one.

7. What the check is for, and what practice converges to

  • The check keeps slips out. With the check, all own samples stay valid. Without it, {{slip_nogate1}}, {{slip_nogate2}} and {{slip_nogate3}} of own samples of 2, 4 and 8 links are valid at the end: an error at one level is built into the next.
  • The check is only as good as the recogniser. With 128 wrong pairs accepted, {{false128_3}} of own chains of 8 links are valid (a product over the links predicts {{false128_pred3}}); with 512, {{false512_3}} ({{false512_pred3}}). Practice never removes a wrong pair. Only teacher chains can.
  • Practice converges to the exact tables. After {{bridge_M}} episodes the midpoint distributions differ from the exact bridge by {{bridge_cond1}} and {{bridge_cond2}} in total variation (spans of 2 and 4 links), and the distance keeps falling with the square root of the number of episodes. 8. Extension: own against foreign examples

In the core model a teacher chain can always be filed, so it is never worse than an own chain. An advantage of own examples needs teacher chains that the Oracle cannot read. This section has two versions. In the first, nothing is given to the Oracle. The second is an earlier version, in which the Oracle is given the step types.

8.1 Nothing given: an Oracle at home in one region

  • A world of regions. The world has 16 regions of 1,024 states, each a world like that of section 1. A step stays inside its region, except at a few states, where one of the {{m}} steps leads into another region. We try three cases: no such state, 1 state in 1,024, and 1 state in 64.
  • The same learner. The Oracle reads a link only if the pair is in its table. It learns by watching and by practice, as in the core model.
  • One restriction on watching. The Oracle can take in a teacher chain only if the chain touches a state it knows. It then learns the whole chain, link by link outwards from that state. A chain that touches nothing it knows is lost.
  • Two stages. First the Oracle watches 2,048 teacher chains that start in one region, its home. Then it gets {{reach_tokens}} tokens (states shown or written), spent on practice, on fresh chains of the whole population, or on half of each.

The fresh chains are exact samples of the population. The Oracle's own chains stay inside what it already reads, so they are further from the population.

  • Fresh chains are lost until the map reaches them. If no state leads to another region, the Oracle takes in {{reach_use0_b0}} of the population's chains, which is the home region's share, and loses the rest for good. With 1 state in 1,024 it still reads less than half of them at the end in {{reach_never_b1024}} of 16 worlds (median {{reach_use_end_b1024}}). With 1 state in 64 it reads {{reach_use0_b64}} at the start, half after {{reach_half_b64}} fresh chains and nine in ten after {{reach_most_b64}}, out of {{reach_fresh_total}}.
  • Practice never enlarges the map. An own chain is made of links the Oracle already accepts. With practice only, the Oracle knows {{reach_own_map_23}} of the world's states at the end, as after the first stage: its home region and the few states beyond it that its teacher chains reached.
  • At home, practice beats fresh chains for long spans. After the {{reach_tokens}} tokens the Oracle writes {{reach_own_home3_23}} of the home region's spans of 8 links if it practised, and {{reach_fresh_home3_23}} if it watched fresh chains (from {{reach_start_home3}} after the first stage). Only 1 fresh chain in 16 is about the home region. This holds whether or not the other chains can be read: a learner that keeps every fresh chain also ends at {{reach_keep_home3_23}}. For spans of 4 links there is no advantage ({{reach_own_home2_20}} against {{reach_fresh_home2_20}} after {{reach_tokens_20}} tokens), because one fresh chain holds five such spans and one own example holds one.
  • For the whole population, fresh chains win once they connect. With 1 state in 64 leading out, fresh chains write half of the population's spans of 4 links after {{reach_fresh_half_pop2}} tokens. Practice alone stays at {{reach_own_pop2_23}}, the home region. If no state leads out, practice wins on the population too, at a low level: {{reach_own_pop3_23_b0}} against {{reach_fresh_pop3_23_b0}} of the spans of 8 links.
  • The restriction costs little when regions connect quickly. With 1 state in 64 leading out, a learner that keeps every fresh chain reaches the same half after {{reach_keep_half_pop2}} tokens.
  • Without regions there is no advantage. In one mixed world of 16,384 states, {{mixed_own_usable_0}} of fresh chains touch the map after the first stage. Practice then writes {{mixed_own_home3_23}} of the spans of 8 links, and fresh chains write {{mixed_fresh_home3_23}}.

What this does and does not show. Own examples are worth more than exact samples of the population where those samples lie outside what the Oracle reads. The advantage stays inside what the Oracle already reads. Practice cannot carry it further, and a fresh chain that the Oracle can read is worth at least as much as an own example. The restriction on watching is an assumption about a bounded learner: one with spare memory could keep the chains it cannot read and connect them later.

8.2 An earlier version, with the step types given

This version gives the Oracle more than it has earned: the step types. Its own chains can then enter states it was never shown, so own examples enlarge the map. With nothing given they cannot (section 8.1). The version changes three things in the core model.

  • Many states, a few step types. The world has 16,384 states. The {{m}} step types act in the same way everywhere, and the Oracle knows them (from a small seed, which we do not model). So it can check any single step, anywhere.
  • A map. The Oracle's tables are keyed by states it knows: those it has produced itself, or reached from a known state by a checked step. It starts with the 9 states of one chain.
  • The filing rule (an assumption). The Oracle can file a chain only if at least one of its states is in the map. The other states of that chain are then reached link by link and join the map. A chain that touches the map nowhere can be checked step by step but not used.

An own example starts at a state of the map and applies 8 known steps. A foreign example is a chain of the population. Both have 9 states and are filed by the same counting rule. Foreign examples are exact samples of the population. Own examples start only inside the map, so they are further from the population.

  • Own examples teach from the start, foreign ones only later. A tenth of the states is in the map after {{map_own_10}} own examples ({{map_own_lo10}}–{{map_own_hi10}} over worlds) or {{map_foreign_10}} foreign ones ({{map_foreign_lo10}}–{{map_foreign_hi10}}), {{map_ratio_10}} times as many. In a world of 65,536 states it is {{big_own_10}} against {{big_foreign_10}}.
  • Foreign chains become usable as the map grows. With a tenth of the states mapped, {{usable_at_10}} of foreign chains touch the map. A map of scattered states would give {{usable_pred_10}}; the real map is made of chains, so its states sit next to each other and are hit together.
  • Writing follows reading. Half of the population's spans of 2 links are written after {{map_own_w1}} own or {{map_foreign_w1}} foreign examples.
  • With few examples, own ones are worth more. Of 256 examples, all own leave {{msplit256_100}} of the states mapped, half and half {{msplit256_50}}, all foreign {{msplit256_0}}. Of 4,096 examples: {{msplit4096_100}}, {{msplit4096_50}} and {{msplit4096_0}}. Once most foreign chains are usable, the two kinds are worth the same.

What this version does and does not show. It shows the size and the time course of an own advantage under the filing rule, and that the map's shape matters. The growth of the map through own examples comes from the given step types. With them, a foreign chain also contains nothing the Oracle could not derive.

9. What the model shows, and what it does not

Shows

  • A recogniser and a generator trained on the same data by the same rule, in which the recogniser is ahead, by a factor that grows with depth and follows a counting law.
  • Generation that improves offline, through generating and checking, to an exact target.
  • Deeper levels arriving later, without a schedule.
  • A check whose errors compound over a span, and practice that cannot repair them.
  • Teacher chains that are lost because they touch nothing the Oracle knows, and own examples that then beat exact samples of the population inside the Oracle's home region (section 8.1).

Does not show

  • Better than fresh data that the Oracle can read. Fresh teacher chains beat practice token for token, unless most of them lie outside what the Oracle reads (section 8.1), or the Oracle may skip at no cost the joins it already holds (section 5).
  • An advantage of judging own samples over judging the teacher's. When the teacher has seeded the generator, the two clean it equally well (section 6).
  • That writing is hard in general. A learner that may iterate its pair table already writes valid chains, one link at a time. The difficulty is specific to writing in one pass, and practice compiles the iteration into the tables.
  • Generalisation. Tables do not generalise across states. The Oracle is the reference for a network, not a model of one.
  • A hidden cause. The recogniser judges correctness; it infers no latent variable.
  • An own advantage beyond what the Oracle already reads. Practice never enlarges the map (section 8.1).
  • A mapping to in-context learning.
