Generative learning · toy model 3 · practice on a chain

A learner that can check a chain long before it can write one, and closes the gap by practice

The task is to generate chains of {{n}} states in which every state fits both of its neighbours, after seeing a teacher's set of such chains. Recognising a valid chain needs one table of allowed pairs. Generating one in a single pass needs a tree of tables that say, for the two ends of a span, which state lies in the middle. A learner made of such tables recognises the whole population after a few hundred teacher chains, but it generates little beyond the chains it was shown: its top table needs about {{lead3}} times as many chains. Practice closes that gap and needs {{saving}} times fewer teacher chains: the learner generates spans itself, its pair table judges them, and one signed rule counts an entry up when its span is accepted and down when it is not. The same rule removes random entries from a generator that starts at random, which teacher chains never do.

1. Task, teacher and learner

Task

There are \(S\) states. The world is a matrix \(\mathbf{T}\in\{0,1\}^{S\times S}\) with \(T_{ab}=1\) if state \(b\) may follow state \(a\). Every state has \(m\) allowed successors. Here \(S\) = {{S}} and \(m\) = {{m}}, and \(\mathbf{T}\) is drawn at random. A chain is \(\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\) with \(n=2^L+1\) states; here \(L=3\), so \(n\) = {{n}}. Each link has a latent \(z_t=T_{x_t x_{t+1}}\in\{0,1\}\), which says whether the link is allowed. A chain is valid if all its links are allowed. The population is the valid chains, with a uniform first state and a uniform choice among the allowed steps:

\[\begin{gathered}\mathbf{x}\ \text{is valid}\iff\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}z_t=1,\\ z_t=T_{x_t x_{t+1}},\\ p(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{S}\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}\frac{T_{x_t x_{t+1}}}{m}.\end{gathered}\]

Two things are asked of a learner.

  • Recognition. Given a chain \(\mathbf{x}\), infer for each link whether it is allowed, \(\hat z_t\), and so whether the chain is valid.
  • Generation. Given the two ends \(x_i=a\) and \(x_{i+2k}=b\) of a span of \(2k\) links taken from a population chain (\(k=1,2,4\)), produce the states in between, \(\tilde x_{i+1},\dots,\tilde x_{i+2k-1}\), in one pass: a fixed number of steps, in which nothing that was generated is fed back in. The span counts as generated if the result is valid.

Teacher, training set and population

The teacher is the ground truth. It recognises with \(\mathbf{T}\) itself and generates with the exact tables given below. It gives the learner a training set of \(N\) chains drawn independently from the population,

\[\mathcal{D}_N=\{\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(N)}\},\qquad \mathbf{x}^{(j)}\sim p.\]

Every score has two versions: on the chains of \(\mathcal{D}_N\), and on the population, measured on held-out chains. The second is generalisation. Cost is counted in tokens: one token is one state shown by the teacher or generated by the learner.

Learner

The learner consists of tables of counts and one rule.

  • Recogniser. A matrix \(\mathbf{C}\): \(C_{ab}\) is the number of links in \(\mathcal{D}_N\) with \(x_t=a\) and \(x_{t+1}=b\). The learner accepts a link if it has seen its pair, \(\hat z_t=\mathbb{1}[C_{x_t x_{t+1}}\ge 1]\). Its verdict on a chain or span is \(v=+1\) if \(\hat z_t=1\) for every link and \(v=-1\) otherwise. This is one look-up per link, at any length.
  • Generator. Tables \(n_k(a,b,c)\ge 0\) for \(k=1,2,4\). The entry \((a,b,c)\) of table \(k\) stands for a span of \(2k\) links with ends \(x_i=a\), \(x_{i+2k}=b\) and middle state \(x_{i+k}=c\). To generate the span, a node draws the middle state in proportion to its counts (below), or uniformly among all states if it holds no entry for \((a,b)\). It hands \((a,c)\) and \((c,b)\) to two nodes of table \(k/2\). Three levels generate the {{n}} states in one top-down pass.
\[\begin{gathered}q_k(\tilde x_{i+k}=c\mid x_i=a,\,x_{i+2k}=b)\\ =\frac{n_k(a,b,c)}{\sum_{c'}n_k(a,b,c')}\end{gathered}\]

The teacher's tables, which generate the population exactly, are

\[\begin{gathered}p(x_{i+k}=c\mid x_i=a,\,x_{i+2k}=b)\\ =\frac{(\mathbf{T}^{k})_{ac}\,(\mathbf{T}^{k})_{cb}}{(\mathbf{T}^{2k})_{ab}},\\ (\mathbf{T}^{2k})_{ab}=\sum_c(\mathbf{T}^{k})_{ac}\,(\mathbf{T}^{k})_{cb},\end{gathered}\]

where \((\mathbf{T}^{k})_{ac}\) is the number of routes of \(k\) links from \(a\) to \(c\). To recognise, the learner has to learn \(\mathbf{T}\) itself, which has \(Sm\) = {{pairs_total}} allowed pairs. To generate in one pass it has to learn the powers of \(\mathbf{T}\): table \(k\) has about \(Sm^{2k}\) spans to cover, which is {{spans1}}, {{spans2}} and {{spans_total}}.

One rule. An entry is tested when the span it stands for is written out, \((\tilde x_i,\dots,\tilde x_{i+2k})\) with \(\tilde x_i=a\), \(\tilde x_{i+k}=c\) and \(\tilde x_{i+2k}=b\), and the recogniser gives its verdict \(v\) on it. Then

\[\begin{gathered}n_k(a,b,c)\;\leftarrow\;\max\{0,\;n_k(a,b,c)+v\},\\ v=v(\tilde x_i,\dots,\tilde x_{i+2k})\in\{+1,-1\}.\end{gathered}\]

Stages of learning

  • Start. \(\mathbf{C}=0\). The generator's tables are empty, or hold random entries (last part of section 3).
  • Stage 1, recognition. The learner counts the links of \(\mathcal{D}_N\) into \(\mathbf{C}\). No generator is involved. In effect this is contrastive: a pair is accepted if it occurs in the teacher set and rejected if it does not, which is what a classifier of the teacher's pairs against random pairs converges to. It is not a recognition-parametrised model, because no hidden cause is inferred; the only latent is \(\hat z_t\).
  • Stage 1, wake. Every span of every teacher chain, with \(a=x_i\), \(c=x_{i+k}\) and \(b=x_{i+2k}\), is an entry tested on the teacher's own writing, so \(v=+1\). The generator is thus fitted to the teacher's samples before any practice. When it fails on the population, it is not for lack of training.
  • Stage 2, practice. No teacher data. Each episode picks one of the three tables at random and tests one entry in one of two ways. Joining: the learner draws a span of \(k\) links that it holds, from \(a\) to \(c\), and a second one that starts where the first ends, from \(c\) to \(b\), each in proportion to its count (a span of one link is an accepted pair; a longer one is the pair of ends of an entry of table \(k/2\)). The entry tested is \((a,b,c)\) of table \(k\), and the learner generates its \(2k+1\) states with its lower tables. Rehearsing: the learner draws an entry it holds, in proportion to its count, and generates its span.

Unless stated otherwise the tables start empty, so there is nothing to prune, and practice consists of joins only. All scores are medians or means over 16 random worlds, with 8,000 held-out population chains each. An own chain is a span of 8 links whose entry the learner draws from its own top table.

2. Stage 1: recognition generalises, generation does not

Both parts of the learner are fitted to the same teacher set by the same rule. After \(N\) chains a given allowed pair is in the set with probability \(1-e^{-N(n-1)/(Sm)}\), and a given span of \(2k\) links with probability \(1-e^{-N(n-2k)/(Sm^{2k})}\), because a chain holds \(n-1\) links but only \(n-2k\) such spans, and there are \(m^{2k-1}\) times as many spans as pairs. So on the population, table \(k\) trails the pair table by the factor

\[\frac{N^{(k)}_{1/2}}{N^{(0)}_{1/2}}=m^{2k-1}\,\frac{n-1}{n-2k},\]

which is {{lead_theory1}}, {{lead_theory2}} and {{lead_theory3}} for spans of 2, 4 and 8 links.

  • Recognition generalises first. The learner accepts half of the population's links after {{half_read0}} teacher chains.
  • Generation is trained, but does not generalise. At every size of the teacher set the learner generates {{watch_train_min}} of the teacher set's own spans (dashed line in panel a). Of the population's spans of 2, 4 and 8 links it generates half only after {{half_watch1}}, {{half_watch2}} and {{half_watch3}} chains, which is {{lead1}}, {{lead2}} and {{lead3}} times later than recognition.
  • Nothing favours recognition except that it has less to learn. The delay follows the counting factor in four other worlds as well (panel b).

3. Stage 2: practice makes generation generalise

A join composes two spans that the learner holds into one that it does not. This samples the second formula for the teacher's tables in section 1, so practice derives the upper tables from the pair table and needs no teacher data.

  • Practice replaces teacher chains. With a teacher set of 4,096 chains the learner accepts every population link but generates only {{n12_w3}} of the population's spans of 8 links. Practice alone brings that to {{n12_p3}}. Half of the spans are generated with a teacher set of {{half_practice}} chains instead of {{half_watch3}}, {{saving}} times fewer.
  • The wake is not what makes practice work. If stage 1 fills the pair table only, practice ends within {{pairs_only_gap}} of the same value (dashed line in panel a).
  • The window in panel b is a Goldilocks zone. The teacher set has to be large enough to complete recognition, and not so large that it has already taught generation. For spans of 8 links, practice to saturation adds more than half of the population's spans for teacher sets between {{gain_window3}} chains.
  • Inside the window, fresh teacher chains still add more than practice. There the teacher is exhausted for recognition but not for generation, so each fresh chain brings new spans: a chain of {{n}} states holds 13 of them, and a join tests one. After a teacher set of 4,096 chains, \(2^{21}\) further tokens of practice bring the population's spans of 8 links from {{win4096_before}} to {{win4096_practice}}, and the same tokens of fresh teacher chains to {{win4096_fresh}}. Practice is ahead at no size of the teacher set (panel c).
  • Own examples win in the window only if the learner does not repeat itself. If it may look a join up first and skip, at no cost, the ones it already holds, practice reaches {{win4096_aim}} and is ahead of fresh chains for teacher sets between {{win_aim_zone}} chains. This selection is not part of the model. Nothing in the learner's own sampling steers it towards the joins it lacks.
  • The levels arrive in order. Population spans of 2, 4 and 8 links are half generated after {{stair12_1}}, {{stair12_2}} and {{stair12_3}} episodes. No schedule is set: a join for table \(k\) needs spans from table \(k/2\).
  • Recognition of longer units grows with generation. An entry is also a unit that the learner can check in one look-up. So the spans it recognises as one unit are the spans it generates (circles on the lines). The links it accepts do not change with practice.
  • The learner generalises to new combinations of links, not to new links. With a teacher set of 512 chains it accepts {{stair_q9}} of the population's links, and practice stops at {{n9_p1}}, {{n9_p2}} and {{n9_p3}} of the spans of 2, 4 and 8 links.
  • Practice needs stage 1 first. The same examples give {{sched_watch_first}} of the spans of 8 links if the teacher set comes first, and {{sched_practise_first}} if practice comes first.

A generator that starts at random

So far the tables started empty. A generator has to start somewhere, so here every table starts with {{rand_rows}} random entries of count 1, almost all of them wrong. Stage 1 is a teacher set of 4,096 chains. After it only {{rand_stage1_own}} of the learner's own chains are valid. In stage 2, practice is half joins and half rehearsals.

  • The signed rule removes random entries, and teacher chains never do. A rehearsed random entry is rejected and loses its count: {{rand_signed_left_end}} of those in the top table are left at the end. Fresh teacher chains do not come from the random entries, so they never test them. They only add right entries, which makes the random ones a smaller share of what the learner draws. Practice that ignores failures does the same.
  • The effect on the learner's own chains is modest. 99% of them are valid after {{rand_signed_t99}} tokens of practice with the signed rule and after {{rand_teacher_t99}} tokens of fresh teacher chains. If failures are ignored, {{rand_plus_own_end}} are valid at the end.
  • It fades for a large teacher set. After \(2^{21}\) tokens of stage 2, {{rn256_signed_own}} of own chains are valid with practice and {{rn256_teacher_own}} with fresh teacher chains if the teacher set had 256 chains. With 65,536 chains the two give {{rn65536_signed_own}} and {{rn65536_teacher_own}}. A large teacher set leaves the random entries a small share of the counts, so they are rarely drawn and rarely tested.

The random entries are an assumption. A table has no bias of its own, and we put them in by hand.

4. A network realisation

This is a proposal; it has not been simulated. Each table becomes a small network, and the signed count becomes a signed gradient step.

  • States. Each state \(a\) has an embedding \(\mathbf{e}_a\in\mathbb{R}^d\), shared by all parts.
  • Recogniser. One network for a pair of neighbours, applied to every link in parallel: \(\hat z_t=\sigma\big(f_\phi(\mathbf{e}_{x_t},\mathbf{e}_{x_{t+1}})\big)\), and \(v=+1\) if \(\hat z_t>\tfrac12\) for every link. It is trained in stage 1 on the teacher set alone, by contrast with pairs whose second state \(b'\) is drawn at random (first formula below). The pair table is its limit, with \(f=+\infty\) for a pair in the teacher set and \(-\infty\) otherwise.
  • Generator. One network per table, shared across positions, with a softmax over the middle state (second formula). The \(L\) networks applied top-down are one pass. For practice the learner also has to propose ends, which the tables do by drawing a span they hold: a second softmax per span length, \(r_{\psi_\ell}(x_{i+\ell}=b\mid x_i=a)\) for \(\ell=1,2,4\) links, fitted by the same rule.
\[\begin{gathered}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}=-\sum_{(a,b)\in\mathcal{D}_N}\Big[\log\sigma\big(f_\phi(\mathbf{e}_a,\mathbf{e}_b)\big)\\ \qquad\qquad{}+\log\big(1-\sigma(f_\phi(\mathbf{e}_a,\mathbf{e}_{b'}))\big)\Big],\\[6pt] q_{\theta_k}(\tilde x_{i+k}=c\mid x_i=a,\,x_{i+2k}=b)\\ =\mathrm{softmax}_c\,g_{\theta_k}(\mathbf{e}_a,\mathbf{e}_b).\end{gathered}\]

One rule. For every node of a generated span, with the verdict \(v\) on that node's span,

\[\begin{gathered}\Delta\theta_k\;\propto\;v\,\nabla_{\theta_k}\log q_{\theta_k}(\tilde x_{i+k}=c\mid a,b),\\ \Delta\mathbf{w}_{c'}\;\propto\;v\,\big(\mathbb{1}[c'=c]-q_{\theta_k}(c'\mid a,b)\big)\,\mathbf{h}.\end{gathered}\]

The second line is the rule for the output weights \(\mathbf{w}_{c'}\) with last hidden layer \(\mathbf{h}\): presynaptic activity, postsynaptic choice, and the verdict as a third factor. On the teacher's spans \(v=+1\), and the rule is maximum likelihood (wake). On own spans it ascends the probability that an own span is accepted, \(\nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}[v]\). The count rule of section 1 is its tabular counterpart. The stages are those of Figure 1. A join draws a start \(a\), proposes \(c\sim r_{\psi_k}(\cdot\mid a)\) and \(b\sim r_{\psi_k}(\cdot\mid c)\), generates the two halves, and updates table \(k\) and the proposal \(r_{\psi_{2k}}\) with the verdict.

We expect three differences from the tables. Random initial weights give the generator spurious mass without our putting it in (section 3). Shared embeddings may let recogniser and generator generalise to pairs outside the teacher set, rightly or wrongly; the tables give the reference for that, namely the counting factors of section 2 and the ceilings of section 3. And if states are seen only through noisy observations, the recogniser needs a readout of the state, which is where a recognition-parametrised model would enter.

5. Limits

  • Generalisation stops at the links of the teacher set. Tables do not generalise across states.
  • Practice does not beat a larger teacher set (Figure 3c). We found two exceptions, each with an added assumption. One is skipping, at no cost, the joins the learner already holds (Figure 3c). The other is a world of 16 regions, where most teacher chains lie outside what the learner recognises: there practice generates {{reach_own_home3_23}} of the home region's spans of 8 links and teacher chains {{reach_fresh_home3_23}}.
  • More teacher chains make the learner worse only if the teacher slips. If it slips on 3% of its states and the generator takes its chains as shown, {{judge_teacher_own3}} of own chains are valid, however many chains follow.
  • The practised tables converge to the teacher's in a world with many routes (within {{bridge_cond2}} in total variation), with joins only.
  • Generation is hard only in one pass. A pair table applied link by link already generates valid chains.
  • There is no hidden cause, and no mapping to in-context learning.
