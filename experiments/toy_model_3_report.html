Generative learning · toy model 3 · the Oracle on a chain

A learner that can check a chain long before it can write one, and closes the gap by practice

The world is a set of chains of {{n}} states in which every state has to fit both of its neighbours. Checking a chain needs one table of allowed pairs. Writing a chain in a single pass needs more: a tree of tables that say, for the two ends of a span, which state lies in the middle. The Oracle, a learner made only of such tables, fills all of them from the same teacher chains by the same counting rule. Its pair table is complete after a few hundred chains, while its top table needs about {{lead3}} times as many. Practice closes the gap without further teacher chains: the Oracle joins pieces it already holds, writes the result in one pass, checks every link against its pair table, and counts what passes.

{{half_practice}} chains teacher chains for the same once the Oracle practises: {{saving}} times fewer
{{map_own_10}} vs {{map_foreign_10}} own against foreign examples until a tenth of a large world is in the Oracle's map (the own-versus-foreign extension, section 8)

Summary

  1. The setup. {{S}} states, each with {{m}} allowed successors; the population is the valid chains of {{n}} states. The Oracle is our name for the learner: a recogniser (one table of pairs) and a generator (a tree of three tables, for spans of 2, 4 and 8 links). It learns by counting, either from teacher chains (watching) or from chains it has assembled, written and checked itself (practice). All results are over 16 random worlds.
  2. Recognition leads, on equal footing. The same chains and the same counting rule fill the recogniser {{lead1}}, {{lead2}} and {{lead3}} times sooner than the generator's three levels. A counting argument predicts {{lead_theory1}}, {{lead_theory2}} and {{lead_theory3}}, and the delay grows with the length of the span in four other worlds as predicted (section 2).
  3. Practice closes the gap without teacher data. After 4,096 teacher chains the Oracle accepts every link but writes only {{n12_w3}} of the population's spans of 8 links. Practice alone brings that to {{n12_p3}}. Counted in teacher chains, half of the spans are written after {{half_practice}} chains instead of {{half_watch3}} (section 3). The practised tables converge to the exact ones (section 7).
  4. Reading and writing rise together, level by level. In practice, spans of 2 links come first, then 4, then 8 (half-way after {{stair12_1}}, {{stair12_2}} and {{stair12_3}} episodes). A span the Oracle can write is also one it holds as a single unit and can check in one lookup, so its reading grows with its writing (section 4).
  5. Watch first, then practise. Practice pays in a window: too few teacher chains and the pair table has gaps that practice cannot fill, too many and watching alone suffices. The same examples in the order watch-then-practise give {{sched_watch_first}}, alternating {{sched_alternate}}, practise-then-watch {{sched_practise_first}} (section 5).
  6. Practice does not beat fresh teacher chains. Token for token, spending the whole budget on fresh teacher chains was best at every budget we tried. Practice is what the Oracle can do when no more teacher chains come (section 5).
  7. Supervising every level adds nothing. A teacher who supplies only the pair table does as well as one who fills every level. With a teacher who slips, a recogniser that accepts every pair it sees ruins the generator ({{noisy_trust_own}} of own chains valid), and a careful one keeps it ({{noisy_left_own}}) (section 6).
  8. Own against foreign examples. In an extension with many states and a few step types that act everywhere, the Oracle can file only chains that touch its map of known states. Own chains always do. Foreign chains are exact samples of the population and mostly do not, until the map has grown. A tenth of the world is mapped after {{map_own_10}} own examples or {{map_foreign_10}} foreign ones. This advantage rests on that filing rule, which is an assumption (section 8).

1. The model

World

There are \(S\) states. Each state has \(m\) allowed successors, which gives a table \(\mathbf{T}\) of allowed pairs, \(T_{ab}=1\) if \(b\) may follow \(a\). The population is the set of chains \(\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\) in which every link \((x_t,x_{t+1})\) is allowed, with a uniform first state and a uniform choice among the allowed successors at every step. So every state has to fit the one before it and the one after it. The main world has \(S\) = {{S}}, \(m\) = {{m}} and \(n\) = {{n}}, so a chain has 8 links.

The Oracle

The Oracle is the learner. It holds tables of counts and nothing else, which makes it the reference against which a network version can later be compared.

  • Recogniser. A pair table \(\hat{\mathbf{T}}\): how often \(b\) was seen right after \(a\). A link passes once its pair has been seen. A chain passes if all of its links do. This is one lookup per link and one AND, at any length.
  • Generator. A binary tree with one table per level. A node holds the two ends \((a,b)\) of a span of \(2k\) links and draws the state \(c\) in the middle from its table. Its two children then hold \((a,c)\) and \((c,b)\). With \(k=4,2,1\) the tree writes the 9 states in one top-down pass, with no loop. A node without an entry for its ends guesses uniformly.

The table a node of half-length \(k\) should hold is the bridge of the chain,

\[\begin{gathered}p_k(c\mid a,b)=\frac{T^{k}(c\mid a)\,T^{k}(b\mid c)}{T^{2k}(b\mid a)},\\ T^{2k}(b\mid a)=\sum_c T^{k}(c\mid a)\,T^{k}(b\mid c),\end{gathered}\]

where \(T^{k}(c\mid a)\) is the probability of being at \(c\) after \(k\) steps from \(a\). The recogniser needs \(\mathbf{T}\) only. The generator needs its powers. They are fixed by \(\mathbf{T}\), but a network that writes in one pass has to store them.

Two ways of learning, one counting rule

  • Watching. A teacher chain adds its 8 adjacent pairs to the recogniser. It also adds every span it contains to the generator: for each \(k\) and each position, a count for (ends, midpoint). This is the maximum-likelihood fit of every table to the supplied chains, the Oracle's version of an autoencoder trained end to end.
  • Practice. No teacher data. One episode picks a level \(k\) at random, takes a unit of \(k\) links that the Oracle holds, \(a\to c\), and a second one that starts where the first ends, \(c\to b\). The Oracle writes the \(2k+1\) states in one pass, checks every link with the recogniser, and adds a count for \((a,b)\to c\) if all links pass. A unit of one link is an accepted pair. A unit of \(2k\) links is a span for which the level-\(k\) table has an entry.

Why this is not learning from what the Oracle already believes. Practice applies the same counting rule as watching, to the Oracle's own chains. A learner that counts plain samples of its own generator learns nothing on average. Here the span is assembled from the levels below and filed in the level above, so the upper table is trained on what the lower tables imply, and that is the second formula above. All information comes from the pair table, which came from the teacher.

What is measured

All scores are on 8,000 held-out population chains per world. A span of the population is written if one pass of the generator from its two ends gives a chain with allowed links only (judged by the world's \(\mathbf{T}\)). It is read as a unit if the Oracle holds an entry for its ends, so that it could check a chain that shows only those two states. A link is accepted if its pair is in the recogniser.

The counting argument

A chain shows \(n-1\) of the \(Sm\) allowed pairs, and \(n-2k\) of about \(S\,m^{2k}\) spans of \(2k\) links. After \(N\) chains the shares seen at least once are about

\[\begin{gathered}q(N)=1-e^{-N(n-1)/(Sm)},\\ r_k(N)=1-e^{-N(n-2k)/(S\,m^{2k})},\end{gathered}\]

so the generator's level \(k\) is half full later than the recogniser by a factor \(m^{2k-1}(n-1)/(n-2k)\). Practice needs every link of a span to be accepted, which happens for a share of about \(q^{2k}\) of the spans.

4. Reading and writing rise together, level by level

  • The levels arrive in order. Spans of 2 links are half written after {{stair12_1}} practice episodes, spans of 4 after {{stair12_2}}, spans of 8 after {{stair12_3}}. Nothing schedules this. A level can only join units that the level below has produced.
  • Writing a span makes it readable as a unit. The share of spans held as one unit and the share written in one pass never differ by more than {{read_write_gap}} in any run (the difference is an occasional lucky guess of a midpoint). In the Oracle they are the same table.
  • The order is a ratchet. Links are accepted first, from the teacher. Checked links certify written spans of 2 links. Those become units of 2 links, which the next level joins, and so on. Recognition is ahead at the bottom only. Above it, each level of reading is made by writing at that level.
  • What the larger units are good for. A chain that shows only every second, fourth or eighth state can then be checked with one lookup per shown link. Before practice the Oracle could check such a chain only where it had watched the very same span.
  • Different routes, one unit. A unit is filed under its two ends. In this world {{multi_route3}} of the spans of 8 links have ends that more than one route joins, and the Oracle holds each of them as a single unit with several midpoints. It finds this out by writing the routes, not by seeing them side by side in the teacher's chains.

5. How much to watch, how much to practise

  • Practice needs a period of watching first. With the same examples, watching and then practising ends at {{sched_watch_first}} of the spans of 8 links, alternating in 64 blocks at {{sched_alternate}}, practising and then watching at {{sched_practise_first}}. Practice on an empty pair table has nothing to join.
  • Teacher chains and practice replace each other only in one direction. Along the bottom row of the heat map (no practice), {{half_watch3}} teacher chains are needed for half of the spans. With a few million practice episodes, {{half_practice}} are. No amount of practice makes up for a pair the teacher has not shown.
  • Token for token, fresh teacher chains are better. With a budget of \(2^{19}\) tokens, spending all of it on teacher chains gives {{split19_all}}, half of it {{split19_half}}. The whole budget for the teacher was best at all four budgets (in {{split15_wins}}, {{split17_wins}}, {{split19_wins}} and {{split21_wins}} of 16 worlds). A teacher chain of 9 states brings spans at every level and new pairs. A practice episode brings one span, and even an episode at the top level, which also writes 9 states, brings only one.

So the benefit of practice in this model is not that own examples are better than the teacher's. It is that they cost no teacher.

6. Supervision at every level, and a teacher who slips

Section 3 already showed one half of this: a teacher who fills every level of the generator achieves nothing that a teacher who supplies only the pair table does not. Here the teacher also slips: 3% of the states in its chains are replaced by random ones. Three learners watch the same chains and then practise.

  • Trusting every pair is costly. A recogniser that accepts a pair seen once has accepted {{noisy_trust_wrong}} wrong pairs after 8,192 chains, next to the {{pairs_total}} right ones. Practice builds on them: only {{noisy_trust_own}} of the Oracle's own chains of 9 states are valid.
  • A careful recogniser protects practice. Accepting a pair only when seen twice leaves {{noisy_forced_wrong}} wrong pairs, and {{noisy_forced_own}} of own chains are valid after practice.
  • Letting the teacher fill every level costs a little. The teacher's slips go straight into the generator's tables: before practice only {{noisy_forced_own0}} of own chains are valid. Practice dilutes them. Leaving the levels to practice ends at {{noisy_left_own}}, higher in {{noisy_left_wins}} of 16 worlds.
  • Care costs teacher chains. Half of the spans of 8 links are written after {{noisy_left_half}} chains by the careful learners, {{noisy_trust_half}} by the trusting one.

7. What the check is for, and what practice converges to

  • The check keeps slips out. With the check, all own samples stay valid. Without it, {{slip_nogate1}}, {{slip_nogate2}} and {{slip_nogate3}} of own samples of 2, 4 and 8 links are valid at the end: an error at one level is built into the next.
  • The check is only as good as the recogniser. With 128 wrong pairs accepted, {{false128_3}} of own chains of 8 links are valid (a product over the links predicts {{false128_pred3}}); with 512, {{false512_3}} ({{false512_pred3}}). Practice never removes a wrong pair. Only teacher chains can.
  • Practice converges to the exact tables. After {{bridge_M}} episodes the midpoint distributions differ from the exact bridge by {{bridge_cond1}} and {{bridge_cond2}} in total variation (spans of 2 and 4 links), and the distance keeps falling with the square root of the number of episodes. In the right panel, {{bridge_multi}} of the {{bridge_entries}} entries have more than one possible midpoint; the correlation with the exact probabilities is {{bridge_corr}}.
  • So practice has an exact target. It is a sampled version of the composition rule in section 1.

8. Extension: own against foreign examples

In the core model a teacher chain can always be filed, so it is never worse than an own chain. This extension changes three things.

  • Many states, a few step types. The world has 16,384 states. The {{m}} step types act in the same way everywhere, and the Oracle knows them (from a small seed, which we do not model). So it can check any single step, anywhere.
  • A map. The Oracle's tables are keyed by states it knows: those it has produced itself, or reached from a known state by a checked step. It starts with the 9 states of one chain.
  • The filing rule (an assumption). The Oracle can file a chain only if at least one of its states is in the map. The other states of that chain are then reached link by link and join the map. A chain that touches the map nowhere can be checked step by step but not used.

An own example starts at a state of the map and applies 8 known steps. A foreign example is a chain of the population. Both have 9 states and are filed by the same counting rule. Foreign examples are exact samples of the population. Own examples start only inside the map, so they are further from the population.

  • Own examples teach from the start, foreign ones only later. A tenth of the states is in the map after {{map_own_10}} own examples ({{map_own_lo10}}–{{map_own_hi10}} over worlds) or {{map_foreign_10}} foreign ones ({{map_foreign_lo10}}–{{map_foreign_hi10}}), {{map_ratio_10}} times as many. In a world of 65,536 states it is {{big_own_10}} against {{big_foreign_10}}.
  • Foreign chains become usable as the map grows. With a tenth of the states mapped, {{usable_at_10}} of foreign chains touch the map. A map of scattered states would give {{usable_pred_10}}; the real map is made of chains, so its states sit next to each other and are hit together.
  • Writing follows reading. Half of the population's spans of 2 links are written after {{map_own_w1}} own or {{map_foreign_w1}} foreign examples.
  • With few examples, own ones are worth more. Of 256 examples, all own leave {{msplit256_100}} of the states mapped, half and half {{msplit256_50}}, all foreign {{msplit256_0}}. Of 4,096 examples: {{msplit4096_100}}, {{msplit4096_50}} and {{msplit4096_0}}. Once most foreign chains are usable, the two kinds are worth the same.

What this does and does not show. The advantage of own examples follows from the filing rule. The run shows its size and its time course, and that the map's shape matters. It does not show that a learner must be unable to store what it cannot read: a learner with spare codes could keep unreadable chains and connect them later. Also, with the step types given, a foreign chain contains nothing the Oracle could not derive. A network version would have to earn both, by reading states it has not produced poorly, and by learning the steps.

9. What the model shows, and what it does not

Shows

  • A recogniser and a generator trained on the same data by the same rule, in which the recogniser is ahead, by a factor that grows with depth and follows a counting law.
  • Generation that improves offline, through generating and checking, to an exact target.
  • Deeper levels arriving later, without a schedule.
  • A check whose errors compound over a span, and practice that cannot repair them.

Does not show

  • Better than fresh data. Fresh teacher chains beat practice token for token.
  • That writing is hard in general. A learner that may iterate its pair table already writes valid chains, one link at a time. The difficulty is specific to writing in one pass, and practice compiles the iteration into the tables.
  • Generalisation. Tables do not generalise across states. The Oracle is the reference for a network, not a model of one.
  • A hidden cause. The recogniser judges correctness; it infers no latent variable.
  • An own advantage without the filing rule (section 8), or any mapping to in-context learning.
