A targeted follow-up: an approximate recognizer

The fully expressive recognizer below showed no sleep advantage. We then tested a standard mean-field recognition family: four independent binary probabilities conditioned on the six input bits, with 28 affine parameters. It cannot represent arbitrary posterior dependencies between causes. Both methods use this same restriction, start from small independent random weights with zero biases, and receive the same finite information. Rates were selected in the separate tuning world and frozen before testing a new fixed world with 12 fresh dataset seeds. We subsequently repeated fully expressive recognition on these exact same datasets and generators to check the effect of capacity directly.

Here sampled sleep improves the real-data learning curve. At N=8,192 its excess loss is 59.1% lower than wake, with a sleep/wake ratio 0.409 [95% seed interval 0.378, 0.445]. The comparison gives each method the same maximum recognition-compute allocation. Wake often stops early after its prescribed training and validation stationarity check, so this headline is not a claim of identical executed compute. Compute check without early stopping

We replayed all 12 seeds at N=512, 2,048 and 8,192 to a budget of 512 wake-update equivalents, before early stopping can save compute. Wake and posterior teaching execute 512 updates; sleep executes 239 combined updates, including 15,296 newly generated contexts, costing 510.59 wake equivalents. Sleep therefore uses 0.28% less dense recognition compute. The shared generator and its fitting cost are identical. These counts cover executed leading matrix products and ancestral generation; they exclude elementwise work and evaluation, so they are an approximate compute proxy. Paired recognition-capacity check

At N=8,192, wake has lower real-data reverse KL (0.140) than sleep (0.148), despite worse observable prediction. This supports a tradeoff between inference objectives under restricted recognition, rather than simply asserting wake failed to optimize its own criterion. It does not certify a global optimum.

This is a learning-curve improvement, not an established change in the asymptotic data exponent. The restricted recognizer leaves an approximation error, and the methods approach different apparent floors. A power fit that ignored those floors would mix statistical learning with approximation bias. The next LLM match should retain an imperfect learned forward model and measure observable prediction through an approximate inverse, with posterior teaching as a control.

Original comparison: a fully expressive affine recognizer

What each method sees

Four hidden binary causes generate eight observed bits through a single sigmoid layer. A complete real example contains eight bits; the task is to predict the last two from the first six. No real latent labels are provided. The generator starts from random, data-independent parameters and is fitted on the training portion by exact marginal MAP optimization. Its selected checkpoint is copied identically to every method, then frozen. Recognition uses a single affine 16-way softmax with small random weights and zero biases. Neither truth weights nor population probabilities enter training or stopping.

Wake replays the finite real contexts. Sleep adds generated cause–context pairs, with known cause labels from the generator's own choices. Posterior teaching uses exact learned-model posteriors on the existing real contexts. Direct prediction trains the observable conditional likelihood using real targets and the same frozen generator. It is a stronger task-directed control, not an additional source of data.

Connection to the weight mechanism

The exact posterior of this one-layer model is affine in the context bits. When the real context design has full column rank, both wake and forward fitting can recover those coefficients from finite contexts. Once they do, observable error comes from the learned generator. Sleep cannot automatically remove that statistical error. In a regular local estimation regime, parameter fluctuations of order N−1/2 yield quadratic KL error of order N−1; this conditional argument predicts a shared leading data exponent after inference catches up. It is not a guarantee of global optimization or a claim about every latent-variable model.

Reproducibility and limits

Hyperparameters were frozen using a separate tuning world and independent seeds, with the same learning-rate grid for every arm. Recognition learning rates decay with cumulative charged compute. Evaluation uses exact held-out population expectations only to avoid measurement noise; all training data and dreams are finite samples. Training stops using finite validation examples already counted in N. Checkpoints include optimizer moments, RNG states and data-stream position. This is a modular model with a frozen learned generator, not yet a joint-training LLM test.
